\chapter{Conclusion and Recommendations}

\section{Summary of Findings}
This research developed and validated a framework for aerothermal analysis and survivability optimization of Hypersonic Inflatable Aerodynamic Decelerators (HIADs) using the SPARTA Direct Simulation Monte Carlo (DSMC) solver. The framework couples parametric CadQuery geometry generation, a 5-species air DSMC simulation, PINN refinement and surrogate-based optimization, and it is validated against IRVE-3 flight data. The study quantified the heating augmentation caused by scalloped surface topology, resolved the local continuum-band structure of the Knudsen field, and delivered a round-1 geometry that passed the hybrid validation gate while exposing the backface-temperature violation of the thermal constraint as the open design issue.

\section{Key Conclusions}

\subsection{DSMC Validation Against IRVE-3 Flight Data}
The SPARTA DSMC framework was validated against IRVE-3 post-flight aerothermal reconstruction data~\cite{old2013irve3postflight,dillman2014irve3flight}. The Fay-Riddell stagnation-point correlation predicts 13.83\,W/cm$^2$ peak heat flux, $+0.2\%$ from the $13.80$\,W/cm$^2$ flight value. The Sutton-Graves correlation~\cite{sutton1971gravesh}, evaluated at the DSMC run with the Earth-air coefficient $C_{SG} = 1.7415 \times 10^{-4}$ and the IRVE-3 nose radius $R_N = 1.5$\,m, gives 15.36\,W/cm$^2$ stagnation-point heat flux. It is $+11.3\%$ relative to flight and $+0.6\%$ relative to the trajectory-integrated Sutton-Graves peak of 15.26\,W/cm$^2$~\cite{rapisarda2023mdao}; the 14.36\,W/cm$^2$ figure of earlier revisions is the recorded group-R reference set reported by Rapisarda~\cite{rapisarda2023mdao}, the campaign reference, not flight.

\subsection{Scalloping-Induced Heating Augmentation}
The toroidal surface topology creates periodic heating with enhanced peaks at the toroid crests. Against the smooth-body run, which sits at a different trajectory condition, the scalloped configuration at the common final step raises peak cell heat flux by 21.2\% and the per-element average by 1.5\%, consistent with the Sutton-Graves $1/\sqrt{R}$ radius scaling. Recirculation zones between toroidal rings provide partial thermal shielding, reducing time-averaged heat load on the FTPS~\cite{hollis2018surface}.

\subsection{Flow Regime Characterization}
The Knudsen number field mapping demonstrates a \emph{local} continuum-band structure around the HIAD at 51.8\,km. Every cell of the computed field lies within the continuum band, spanning nearly three decades from $1.7\times10^{-6}$ to $9.75\times10^{-4}$; compression behind the bow shock lowers $Kn$ while the far field carries the highest cell-scale values. Neither the slip band ($0.01 \lesssim Kn \lesssim 0.1$), the transitional band ($0.1 \lesssim Kn \lesssim 10$) nor the free-molecular band ($Kn > 10$) is attained at cell scale. The kinetic treatment remains justified by scale: $Kn$ is a local ratio, and over the millimetre bow-shock thickness the mean free path of order $10^{-4}$\,m yields $Kn$ of order $0.01$ to $0.1$, inside the slip regime (Chapter~\ref{chap:literature}), where continuum Navier-Stokes needs slip corrections~\cite{bird1994molecular, plimpton2014sparta}. A vehicle-scale ratio ($Kn \approx 3.4\times10^{-5}$ on the 3\,m aeroshell) would conceal this structure, so the local field, resolved across its nearly three decades, is the meaningful diagnostic: the continuum PINN and kinetic DSMC stages are complementary.

\subsection{Grid Independence and Computational Efficiency}
A grid factor of 0.7 best balances accuracy and cost, with the least error against validated flight data. The Cartesian grid at a grid factor of 0.7 resolves the bow-shock thickness and the near-wall gradients with adequate particles per cell, satisfying DSMC resolution.

\subsection{Real-Gas Effects and 5-Species Air Model}
A realistic 5-species air model (N$_2$, O$_2$, NO, N, O) with Variable Soft Sphere (VSS) collision physics captures vibrational excitation and dissociation, reducing the effective stagnation temperature below the calorically ideal prediction of 5952\,K. Energy partitioning into internal modes affects the temperature gradient driving surface heat transfer~\cite{AndersonHypersonic}.

\subsection{Optimization and Thermal Constraint}
The optimization work evaluated a 15-point central composite screen~\cite{Montgomery2017} and, separately, a 70-entry Bayesian Optimization campaign (20 Latin Hypercube designs and 50 Expected-Improvement iterations)~\cite{sklearn2024gp} across three design variables (nose radius, toroid radius, half-cone angle). The multi-objective cost balances ballistic coefficient, peak deceleration and backface temperature against total heat load, and the campaign converged toward a larger-nose design that then passed the DSMC validation gate of Chapter~\ref{ch:results}.

The thermal constraint is violated: the active TPS reports a backface temperature of 955.66\,K, exceeding the Kapton limit of 773\,K by 182.7\,K~\cite{old2013irve3postflight}. The system is the five-ply IRVE-3 flexible TPS (two 0.60\,mm Nextel BF-20 outer fabric plies, two 2.50\,mm Pyrogel 2250 insulation plies, one 0.15\,mm aluminized Kapton/Kevlar gas barrier), totaling 6.35\,mm with series resistance $R = 0.25605$\,m$^2$\,K/W and outer emissivity 0.80. Survival is judged on four fronts: surface temperature under 2073\,K, backface temperature under 773\,K, and both measured and computed deceleration under 25\,g. The thermal channel binds; the deceleration channel passes with margin.

\section{Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Integrated DSMC-HIAD Framework:} A complete pipeline from parametric geometry through DSMC simulation to survivability optimization for inflatable decelerators.

    \item \textbf{Scalloping Effect Quantification:} DSMC-based characterization of heating augmentation and recirculation patterns caused by the stacked toroid surface topology of HIADs.

    \item \textbf{PINN-DSMC Hybrid Approach:} Physics-Informed Neural Networks as a smoothing layer for DSMC particle data, anchored in the compressible Navier-Stokes equations.

    \item \textbf{Constrained Single-Objective Optimization:} Automated surrogate-based optimization of HIAD geometry with Bayesian Optimization (Gaussian Process surrogate, Mat\'ern 5/2 kernel, Expected Improvement) and the engine's Genetic Algorithm search, targeting ballistic coefficient, peak deceleration, backface temperature, and drag performance.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Recommendations for Future Work}

\subsection{Extended Trajectory Analysis}
The study covers one trajectory point at 51.8\,km. Extend it to a full re-entry trajectory, capturing time-evolution of aerothermal loads across conditions and flow regimes.

\subsection{Full 3D Off-Axis Simulations}
Results come from 2D axisymmetric cases at zero angle of attack; full 3D runs at non-zero angle of attack would capture asymmetric heating and side forces relevant to vehicle stability.

\subsection{Structural FSI Coupling}
The HIAD is treated as rigid and fully inflated. Coupling DSMC aerothermal loads to a structural finite element model would enable fluid-structure interaction (FSI) analysis of deformation and its flow-field effects.

\subsection{Gen-2 and Gen-3 TPS Material Assessment}
The baseline is the IRVE-3 Gen-1 TPS (Nextel/Pyrogel). Evaluate thermal margins for Gen-2 (SiC/Carbon) and Gen-3 TPS materials under the predicted scalloped heating profiles, especially for Mars entry, where atmospheric composition differs~\cite{Guidotti2023EFESTO2}.

\subsection{Mars Entry Extension}
Extend the framework to Mars entry with a CO$_2$-dominated composition and updated SPARTA gas model. Replace the Sutton-Graves correlation with the West and Brandis (2020) Mars correlation and the 5-species air model with a CO$_2$/N$_2$/Ar mixture.

\subsection{PINN Refinement Validation}
PINN refinement gives smooth, physics-consistent interpolation of noisy DSMC data, but its accuracy across the continuum and slip-flow bands ($Kn \lesssim 0.1$) needs quantification. Compare raw and PINN-refined outputs across Knudsen numbers to establish reliability bounds.
