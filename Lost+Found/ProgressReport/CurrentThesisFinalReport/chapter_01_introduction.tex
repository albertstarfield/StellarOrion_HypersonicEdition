\chapter{Introduction}
\section{Background}
\label{ch:background}
Space exploration drives technological innovation, yet safe return remains a challenge: payloads must reach Earth or other bodies with an atmosphere. At hypersonic speed, aerodynamic heating can cause catastrophic failure, so a thermal protection system (TPS) is required.
\par
Rigid TPS have been the standard solution for Earth reentry: Reusable TPS, Structurally Integrated TPS, Hot Structures, and Ablators~\cite{TPS-stateofIndustryNASA}. A rigid heat shield is limited by the launch vehicle's payload fairing, restricting reentry vehicle size and mass, which constrains missions landing large payloads such as human Mars missions or assets returned from space.

\par
Deployable entry systems known as inflatable aerodynamic decelerators (IADs), or hypersonic inflatable aerodynamic decelerators (HIADs), have been tested as flexible TPS~\cite{DiNonnoLOFTID}. These structures pack into a small launch volume and inflate before atmospheric entry. Their large surface area generates drag, decelerating heavy payloads high in the atmosphere where heating is less severe.

Inflatable Aerodynamic Decelerator (IAD) development combines aerodynamics, structural mechanics, and materials science. Its key component, the flexible thermal protection system (FTPS), shields the inflatable structure from reentry heat: an outer fabric, insulation, and a gas barrier~\cite{Guo2011Polyimide}. Its design requires accurate modeling of aerothermal loads and material response.

\section{Problem Statement}
IAD design depends on accurate prediction of the aerothermodynamic environment and the fluid-structure interaction (FSI) of atmospheric reentry. Flexible IAD and FTPS structures add complexity absent from rigid heat shields: aerodynamic loading deforms the structure, altering the flow field, heat transfer, and structural response.

At hypersonic speeds ($Ma > 5$) and high altitudes ($> 50$\,km), atmospheric density is low enough that the continuum assumption behind traditional Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) is only locally valid. The Knudsen number ($Kn$) is a \emph{local} ratio of mean free path to characteristic length. The length scale differs by orders of magnitude between the full vehicle and the millimeter-scale shock layer, so a vehicle-wide $Kn$ misrepresents the flow. The freestream stays continuum on the aeroshell scale while the shock layer rises into the slip-flow band, where Navier-Stokes requires wall slip corrections. Particle-based methods such as Direct Simulation Monte Carlo (DSMC) capture the gas dynamics and remain valid at every $Kn$~\cite{bird1994molecular}.

Optimizing HIAD geometry for survivability (balancing drag performance, peak heating, ballistic coefficient, and structural mass) requires an automated framework over a high-dimensional design space, since parametric CFD sweeps are prohibitive at thousands of evaluations.

An integrated framework is required: (1) DSMC rarefied gas dynamics to predict aerothermal loads in the locally non-continuum shock layer, (2) automated parametric geometry generation, and (3) physics-informed machine learning with surrogate-based optimization to identify optimal HIAD configurations.

\section{Research Objective}
The objective is to develop and validate a computational framework for aerothermal analysis and survivability optimization of a Hypersonic Inflatable Aerodynamic Decelerator (HIAD), based on the SPARTA DSMC solver~\cite{plimpton2014sparta}. It integrates physics-informed neural network (PINN) refinement~\cite{raissi2019pinn} and genetic algorithm optimization~\cite{Goldberg1989}.

Specific objectives:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Parametric Geometry Generation:} To develop an automated 3D CAD pipeline using CadQuery that generates HIAD profiles from IRVE-3 specifications, varying toroid count, cone angle, nose radius, and aeroshell diameter.

    \item \textbf{DSMC Aerothermal Analysis:} To perform high-fidelity rarefied gas dynamics simulations using the SPARTA DSMC solver, capturing Boltzmann-level molecular collisions and transport phenomena in the locally non-continuum shock layer at HIAD reentry altitudes of 50-60~km.

    \item \textbf{Knudsen Number Field Mapping:} To analyze the spatial distribution of the Knudsen number across the HIAD surface and flow field, resolving the continuum ($Kn \lesssim 0.01$) and slip-flow ($0.01 \lesssim Kn \lesssim 0.1$) bands that bound the shock-layer gradient. This characterizes the flow physics that governs aerothermal loads.

    \item \textbf{Aerothermal Load Prediction:} To determine the surface distributions of heat flux ($\dot{q}$), pressure, and shear stress using the Sutton-Graves stagnation-point correlation~\cite{sutton1971gravesh} and SPARTA surface compute quantities, validated against IRVE-3 flight data.

    \item \textbf{PINN Flow Field Refinement:} To apply Physics-Informed Neural Networks (DeepXDE) post-processing to smooth noisy DSMC data, fill flow-field gaps, and provide a continuous interpolant anchored in the compressible Navier-Stokes equations.

    \item \textbf{Survivability Optimization:} To implement a Genetic Algorithm (GA) coupled with Bayesian Optimization using a GP Matérn 5/2 surrogate and Expected Improvement~\cite{matern1960,morris1992} to optimize HIAD geometry, targeting ballistic coefficient, peak deceleration, backface temperature, and drag performance.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Research Gap}
Four gaps remain in HIAD aerothermal analysis:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Rarefied Regime Limitation:} Existing HIAD aerothermal studies rely on continuum CFD (RANS/LES), inappropriate for the transitional flow regime ($Kn > 0.01$) above 50\,km. DSMC methods have not been systematically applied to HIAD geometry optimization.

    \item \textbf{Automated Design Space Exploration:} Manual parametric sweeps with individual CFD runs make high-dimensional design optimization (6+ parameters) infeasible. No framework couples automated geometry generation with DSMC simulation and machine learning-based optimization.

    \item \textbf{PINN-DSMC Hybrid Approach:} Physics-Informed Neural Networks have been applied to CFD post-processing, but not as a smoothing layer for noisy DSMC particle data in HIAD applications.

    \item \textbf{Multi-Objective Survivability Optimization:} Simultaneous optimization of ballistic coefficient, peak heating, backface temperature, and structural mass for HIAD configurations with surrogate-assisted genetic algorithms has not been demonstrated.
\end{itemize}

\section{Novelty and Contributions}
This work makes the following novel contributions:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{First Integrated DSMC-PINN-Optimization Framework for HIAD:} This study presents the first framework coupling parametric geometry generation (CadQuery), high-fidelity DSMC rarefied gas dynamics~\mbox{\cite{plimpton2014sparta}}, Physics-Informed Neural Network refinement~\cite{lu2021deepxde}, and surrogate-assisted genetic algorithm optimization for HIAD survivability analysis.

    \item \textbf{DSMC-Based Design Space Exploration:} Unlike prior continuum CFD (RANS/LES) studies, this framework employs particle-based DSMC to capture the locally non-continuum shock layer above 50~km, enabling physically correct aerothermal loads for inflatable decelerators.

    \item \textbf{PINN as DSMC Post-Processor:} Using Physics-Informed Neural Networks as a smoothing and interpolation layer for noisy DSMC particle data is novel in HIAD aerothermal analysis. The two-stage training protocol (data-only, then data+PDE) yields a physics-consistent continuous field from discrete particles.

    \item \textbf{Multi-Objective Surrogate-Assisted Optimization:} Simultaneous optimization of ballistic coefficient, peak heating, backface temperature, and drag performance with Bayesian Optimization (GP surrogate) coupled to a genetic algorithm demonstrates an efficiency that direct simulation alone cannot afford.

    \item \textbf{Comprehensive ML Documentation Standards:} The framework follows machine learning standards: explicit architecture documentation, reproducibility requirements (random seeds, environment specifications), validation metrics ($R^2$, RMSE, MAE), and ensemble uncertainty quantification.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Scope and Limitations}

This research provides aerothermal analysis and survivability optimization of the Flexible Thermal Protection System (FTPS) within available computational resources. Its boundaries are:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Geometric Representation:} The HIAD structure uses parametric 3D CAD generation via CadQuery. The 6-toroid stack based on the MDAO baseline~\cite{rapisarda2023mdao} is the primary geometry, with variable toroid count, cone angle, and nose radius. The ``scalloped'' toroidal surface is treated as rigid and fully inflated to isolate surface-topology effects on the flow.

    \item \textbf{Dimensionality:} Simulations are 2D axisymmetric (via \texttt{-{}-slice-angle}) or full 3D. The 2D mode assumes zero angle of attack ($\alpha = 0^\circ$) to maintain rotational symmetry and raise particle resolution within memory limits; full 3D covers off-axis conditions.

    \item \textbf{Numerical Framework:} Gas dynamics follow the Boltzmann equation, solved by the Direct Simulation Monte Carlo (DSMC) method in the SPARTA (Sandia Parallel Algorithm for Real-Time Application) solver~\cite{plimpton2014sparta}, valid for rarefied and transitional flows where the continuum assumption fails ($Kn > 0.01$).

    \item \textbf{Gas Model:} A realistic 5-species air model (N$_2$, O$_2$, NO, N, O) with Variable Soft Sphere (VSS) collision physics captures molecular interactions including vibrational energy exchange and dissociation reactions relevant to hypersonic entry.

    \item \textbf{Grid and Collision Pairing:} A Cartesian grid restricts collision pairing to $O(N)$ complexity and provides sampling volumes for macroscopic property accumulation. Grid resolution must be below the local mean free path ($\lambda$); the default grid factor is 0.7, validated against IRVE-3 flight data.

    \item \textbf{Thermal Analysis:} Surface heat flux uses the Sutton-Graves stagnation-point correlation~\cite{sutton1971gravesh}, validated against IRVE-3 flight data. The 1D transient backface model assumes a thermal lag factor ($\eta_{lag} = 0.15$) through the FTPS insulation stack. SPARTA surface kinetic energy quantities serve as shape-ranking signals for the optimizer, not absolute heat flux.

    \item \textbf{Optimization:} The Genetic Algorithm explores the design space with Latin Hypercube Sampling (LHS) initial populations and a Gaussian Process (Matérn 5/2) Bayesian Optimization surrogate; PINN refinement using DeepXDE runs during exploration but is bypassed during final validation to eliminate neural network bias.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{a. Literature Study}
The literature review covers hypersonic aerothermodynamics, rarefied gas dynamics, DSMC methodology, and HIAD design: the Boltzmann equation, the Direct Simulation Monte Carlo method~\cite{bird1994molecular}, the Sutton-Graves correlation, and IRVE-3 and LOFTID flight results.

\subsection*{b. Parametric Geometry Generation}
CadQuery, a Python parametric CAD library, generates 3D HIAD models. Aeroshell diameter, forebody cone angle, toroid count, toroid radius, and nose radius are command-line arguments, so hundreds of variants export as STL for SPARTA.

\subsection*{c. DSMC Simulation}
SPARTA runs the DSMC simulation in a containerized Linux environment at the target trajectory point ($v = 3378$\,m/s, $\rho = 7.851 \times 10^{-4}$ kg/m$^3$, $T = 268.41$\,K at 51.8\,km). Millions of particles are advected, collided (VSS model), and sampled at surfaces, where computes accumulate force and energy; grid dumps give density, temperature, pressure, velocity, and Knudsen number fields.

\subsection*{d. PINN Refinement}
A Physics-Informed Neural Network (PINN) in DeepXDE post-processes raw DSMC output, solving the 2D compressible Navier-Stokes equations by automatic differentiation with SPARTA grid data as constraints. Its loss combines PDE residuals with data-matching terms, giving a physics-consistent interpolant that fills flow-field gaps and reduces particle noise.

\subsection*{e. Survivability Optimization}
A Genetic Algorithm (GA) with Bayesian Optimization (GP Matérn 5/2 and Expected Improvement) optimizes HIAD geometry. Latin Hypercube Sampling generates the initial population, the GP surrogate screens candidates, and costly SPARTA runs are reserved for elites and validation; the cost function balances ballistic coefficient, peak deceleration, backface temperature, and drag performance against targets.

\subsection*{f. Validation and Analysis}
Validation against IRVE-3 flight data~\cite{rapisarda2023mdao} uses the Sutton-Graves correlation. Peak heat flux is 14.36\,W/cm$^2$ in flight, 13.83\,W/cm$^2$ from the MDAO Fay-Riddell reference, and 16.14\,W/cm$^2$ from the Sutton-Graves correlation at the present DSMC run. Total heat load is 195.06, 195.17, and 165.72\,J/cm$^2$ respectively; peak deceleration is 19.7\,g in flight against 16.83\,g from this work. Solver-resolved quantities under-predict flight by a common $15\%$ because one trajectory point replaces a full reentry history, while the correlation over-predicts by $+12.4\%$ because it resolves neither the scalloped geometry nor the rarefied departure; both deviations appear in Chapter IV.
