\chapter{Introduction}
\section{Background}
\label{ch:background}
Space exploration drives technological innovation, yet safe return remains a challenge: payloads must reach Earth or other bodies with an atmosphere. At hypersonic speed, aerodynamic heating can cause catastrophic failure, so a thermal protection system (TPS) is required.
\par
Rigid TPS designs have been the standard solution for Earth reentry: Reusable TPS, Structurally Integrated TPS, Hot Structures, and Ablators~\cite{TPS-stateofIndustryNASA}. A rigid heat shield is limited by the launch vehicle's payload fairing, restricting reentry vehicle size and mass, which constrains missions landing large payloads such as human Mars missions or assets returned from space.

\par
Deployable entry systems known as inflatable aerodynamic decelerators (IADs), or hypersonic inflatable aerodynamic decelerators (HIADs), have been tested as flexible TPS~\cite{dinonno2026loftid}. These structures pack into a small launch volume and inflate before atmospheric entry. Their large surface area generates drag, decelerating heavy payloads high in the atmosphere where heating is less severe.

Inflatable Aerodynamic Decelerator (IAD) development combines aerodynamics, structural mechanics, and materials science. Its key component, the flexible thermal protection system (FTPS), shields the inflatable structure from reentry heat: an outer fabric, insulation, and a gas barrier~\cite{Guo2011Polyimide}. Its design requires accurate modeling of aerothermal loads and material response.

\section{Problem Statement}
IAD design depends on accurate prediction of the aerothermodynamic environment and the fluid-structure interaction (FSI) of atmospheric reentry. Flexible IAD and FTPS structures add complexity absent from rigid heat shields: aerodynamic loading deforms the structure, altering the flow field, heat transfer, and structural response.

At hypersonic speeds ($Ma > 5$) and high altitudes ($> 50$\,km), atmospheric density is low enough that the continuum assumption behind traditional Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) is only locally valid. The Knudsen number ($Kn$) is a \emph{local} ratio of mean free path to characteristic length. The length scale differs by orders of magnitude between the full vehicle and the millimeter-scale shock layer, so a vehicle-wide $Kn$ misrepresents the flow. The freestream stays continuum on the aeroshell scale while the shock layer rises into the slip-flow band, where Navier-Stokes requires wall slip corrections. Particle-based methods such as Direct Simulation Monte Carlo (DSMC) capture the gas dynamics and remain valid at every $Kn$~\cite{bird1994molecular}.

Optimizing HIAD geometry for survivability (balancing drag performance, peak heating, and ballistic coefficient) requires an automated framework over a high-dimensional design space, since parametric CFD sweeps are prohibitive at thousands of evaluations.

Specific objectives:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Parametric Geometry Generation:} To develop an automated parametric geometry pipeline that generates HIAD profiles from IRVE-3 specifications, varying toroid count, cone angle, nose radius, and aeroshell diameter; the engine emits the geometry parametrically and writes the solver input deck directly, with no external CAD model and no STL handoff.

    \item \textbf{DSMC Aerothermal Analysis:} To perform high-fidelity rarefied gas dynamics simulations using the SPARTA DSMC solver, capturing Boltzmann-level molecular collisions and transport phenomena in the locally non-continuum shock layer at HIAD reentry altitudes of 50-60~km.

    \item \textbf{Knudsen Number Field Mapping:} To analyze the spatial distribution of the Knudsen number across the HIAD surface and flow field, resolving the continuum-band ($Kn \lesssim 0.01$) gradient of the shock layer and quantifying its distance to the slip-flow ($0.01 \lesssim Kn \lesssim 0.1$) boundary. This characterizes the flow physics that governs aerothermal loads.

    \item \textbf{Aerothermal Load Prediction:} To determine the surface distributions of heat flux ($\dot{q}$), pressure, and shear stress using the Sutton-Graves stagnation-point correlation~\cite{sutton1971gravesh} and SPARTA surface compute quantities, validated against IRVE-3 flight data.

    \item \textbf{PINN Flow Field Refinement:} To apply Physics-Informed Neural Networks (DeepXDE) post-processing to smooth noisy DSMC data, fill flow-field gaps, and provide a continuous interpolant anchored in the compressible Navier-Stokes equations.

\section{Research Gap}
Four gaps remain in HIAD aerothermal analysis:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Rarefied Regime Limitation:} Existing HIAD aerothermal studies rely on continuum CFD (RANS/LES), which is inappropriate once the local Knudsen number reaches the slip band ($Kn > 0.01$). DSMC methods have not been systematically applied to HIAD geometry optimization.

    \item \textbf{Automated Design Space Exploration:} Manual parametric sweeps with individual CFD runs make high-dimensional design optimization (6+ parameters) infeasible. No framework couples automated geometry generation with DSMC simulation and machine learning-based optimization.

    \item \textbf{PINN-DSMC Hybrid Approach:} Physics-Informed Neural Networks have been applied to CFD post-processing, but not as a smoothing layer for noisy DSMC particle data in HIAD applications.

    \item \textbf{Survivability Optimization:} Simultaneous optimization of ballistic coefficient, peak heating, and backface temperature for HIAD configurations with surrogate-assisted optimization has not been demonstrated; this study delivers a constrained single-objective treatment of that problem, so the multi-objective generalization remains open.
\end{itemize}

\section{Scope and Limitations}

This research provides aerothermal analysis and survivability optimization of the Flexible Thermal Protection System (FTPS) within the results it is able to represent at present. The boundaries below state what the delivered results cover and where representation stops.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Geometric Representation:} The results represent the engine-parametric HIAD, written directly to the solver deck with no external CAD model and no STL handoff: the 6-toroid stack on the multidisciplinary design analysis and optimization (MDAO) baseline~\cite{rapisarda2023mdao}, with variable toroid count, cone angle and nose radius. \emph{Not represented:} wall deformation. The ``scalloped'' toroidal surface is held rigid and fully inflated, so the results isolate surface-topology effects and exclude the coupled inflation and deformation response.

    \item \textbf{Dimensionality:} The results represent the 2D axisymmetric half-plane at zero angle of attack (via the \texttt{slice-angle} option), which maximizes particle resolution within memory limits. \emph{Not represented:} off-axis conditions. Full 3D is available in the framework but is not the reported result.

    \item \textbf{Numerical Framework:} The results represent rarefied and transitional gas dynamics from the Boltzmann equation, solved by the Direct Simulation Monte Carlo (DSMC) method in the SPARTA (Stochastic PArallel Rarefied-gas Time-accurate Analyzer, Sandia National Laboratories) solver~\cite{plimpton2014sparta}. \emph{Not represented:} a continuum CFD solution. The continuum equations enter this work only through the PINN stage (Item~7), never as a continuum solve; at vehicle scale the computed field is continuum while the shock-layer scale is not, as Chapter~\ref{ch:results} shows.

    \item \textbf{Gas Model:} The results represent a 5-species air model (N$_2$, O$_2$, NO, N, O) with Variable Soft Sphere (VSS) collision physics, capturing vibrational energy exchange and dissociation relevant to hypersonic entry. \emph{Not represented:} non-air compositions and volumetric shock-layer radiation. Only grey-body surface re-radiation is modelled.

    \item \textbf{Grid and Collision Pairing:} A Cartesian grid restricts collision pairing to $O(N)$ complexity and provides sampling volumes for macroscopic property accumulation. Grid resolution must resolve the bow-shock thickness and near-wall gradients, with cells of order $10^{-1}$\,m three orders of magnitude above the local mean free path of order $10^{-4}$\,m, as DSMC statistical cell sampling permits; the default grid factor is 0.7, validated against IRVE-3 flight data.

    \item \textbf{Thermal Analysis:} The results represent a single-point Sutton-Graves stagnation-point flux~\cite{sutton1971gravesh}, validated against IRVE-3 flight data, and a 1D transient backface through the FTPS insulation stack with thermal lag factor $\eta_{lag} = 0.15$; SPARTA surface kinetic-energy quantities serve as shape-ranking signals, not absolute heat flux. \emph{Not represented:} a full transient in-depth solve and full-trajectory integration. The per-element solve pins the cold face, so the reported backface temperature restates that boundary condition rather than predicting it independently, and the two series sit at their own sampled trajectory states, leaving a shared-state comparison to future work.

% GA de-documented (Genetic Algorithm not used in the recorded run); original:     \item \textbf{Optimization:} Bayesian Optimization explores the design space from a Latin Hypercube Sampling (LHS) initial design on a Gaussian Process (Matérn 5/2) surrogate~\cite{rasmussen2003}, with the Genetic Algorithm search available over the same surrogate; PINN refinement using DeepXDE runs during exploration but is bypassed during final validation to eliminate neural network bias.
    \item \textbf{Optimization:} The results represent a constrained single-objective Bayesian search (LHS initialization, Gaussian Process Matérn 5/2 surrogate~\cite{rasmussen2003}, Expected Improvement) with PINN refinement bypassed at final validation to eliminate neural network bias. \emph{Not represented:} multi-objective optimization, structural mass, and shared-condition coupling among runs.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{a. Literature Study}
The literature review covers hypersonic aerothermodynamics, rarefied gas dynamics, DSMC methodology, and HIAD design: the Boltzmann equation, the Direct Simulation Monte Carlo method~\cite{bird1994molecular}, the Sutton-Graves correlation, and IRVE-3 and LOFTID flight results.

\subsection*{b. Parametric Geometry Generation}
The engine generates the HIAD geometry parametrically in Ada. Aeroshell diameter, forebody cone angle, toroid count, toroid radius, and nose radius are command-line arguments, so hundreds of variants are written directly as SPARTA input decks, with no CAD model and no STL handoff.

\subsection*{c. DSMC Simulation}
SPARTA runs the DSMC simulation in a containerized Linux environment at the target trajectory point ($v = 3378$\,m/s, $\rho = 7.851 \times 10^{-4}$ kg/m$^3$, $T = 268.41$\,K at 51.8\,km). Millions of particles are advected, collided (VSS model), and sampled at surfaces, where computes accumulate force and energy; grid dumps give density, temperature, pressure, velocity, and Knudsen number fields.

\subsection*{d. PINN Refinement}
A Physics-Informed Neural Network (PINN) in DeepXDE post-processes raw DSMC output, solving the 2D compressible Navier-Stokes equations by automatic differentiation with SPARTA grid data as constraints. Its loss combines PDE residuals with data-matching terms, giving a physics-consistent interpolant that fills flow-field gaps and reduces particle noise.

\subsection*{f. Validation and Analysis}
Validation against IRVE-3 flight data uses the Sutton-Graves correlation. Peak heat flux is 13.80\,W/cm$^2$ in the NASA flight reconstruction~\cite{old2013irve3postflight,dillman2014irve3flight}, 13.83\,W/cm$^2$ from the MDAO Fay-Riddell reference, and 15.36\,W/cm$^2$ from the Sutton-Graves correlation with the Earth-air coefficient in the present DSMC run; the values 14.36\,W/cm$^2$ and 195.06\,J/cm$^2$ that earlier revisions carried in the flight column are the recorded group-R reference values reported by Rapisarda~\cite{rapisarda2023mdao}, the reference set the recorded campaigns were fitted against. Total heat load is 188.00, 195.17, and 165.72\,J/cm$^2$, respectively; peak deceleration is 19.7\,g in flight against 16.55\,g raw (16.58\,g PINN-extrapolated) from this work. Flight-integrated quantities under-predict by $-11.9\%$ (heat load) and $-16.0\%$ (deceleration) because one trajectory point replaces a full reentry history, while the correlation over-predicts by $+11.3\%$ because it resolves neither the scalloped geometry nor the rarefied departure, and sits only $+0.6\%$ above Rapisarda's trajectory-integrated Sutton-Graves peak of 15.26\,W/cm$^2$; both deviations appear in Chapter~\ref{ch:results}.
