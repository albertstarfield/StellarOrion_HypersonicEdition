\chapter{Results and Discussion}
\label{ch:results}

\section{Overview}
All results use the StellarOrion engine for the IRVE-3 configuration and SPARTA DSMC framework of Chapter~\ref{ch:methodology}, on 6-toroid stacked HIAD geometry at MDAO baseline parameters~\cite{rapisarda2023mdao}.

\section{Simulation Restriction}
\label{sec:sim_restriction}

The results of this chapter were produced under a hard resource restriction, and that restriction shaped the modelling choices of Chapter~\ref{ch:methodology} as much as any physical consideration. A single SPARTA DSMC simulation of the scalloped geometry advances $O(10^3)$ particle-time steps and costs $O(1)$ wall-clock hour~\cite{plimpton2014sparta} before geometry generation and post-processing, and a direct optimisation loop over the design space needs $O(10^2)$ to $O(10^3)$ such runs. Over the months available to the project that total is not affordable, so the campaign was confined to two workstations: an Apple A2779 (ARM, APL1113/T6020, 6 performance plus 4 efficiency cores, 16\,GB unified memory) and an AMD Ryzen~5~5700 (16 threads, 32\,GB RAM). The solver is therefore kept out of the optimisation loop and the full transient is never resolved.

\subsection{Numerical Constriction}
The constriction is one of computational cost, not of method. The DSMC timestep is fixed at $\Delta t = 1\times10^{-6}$\,s by the mean collision time of the rarefied freestream (Sec.~\ref{sec:timestep}), so the number of particle-time steps, not their size, sets the runtime, and every step costs wall-clock time on a few-core machine~\cite{bird1994molecular}. Three levers move that cost: the CPU clock speed, which shortens every step most directly; the core count, across which SPARTA partitions the domain; and the memory and disk footprint, which set the finest surface grid that can be held and written at each step. With a few cores and 16\,GB to 32\,GB of memory the reduction axes are fixed, namely the governing equations, the domain dimensionality, the transient length, and the number of expensive solver calls.

\subsection{Reductions Applied}
One reduction is applied on each axis:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Analytic surrogate inside the loop.} The optimiser scores each candidate with the closed-form cost function of Sec.~\ref{sec:cost_function}, following Rapisarda's MDAO reconstruction~\cite{rapisarda2023mdao}, instead of a DSMC call, so the 70-evaluation search of Sec.~\ref{sec:bo_results} costs seconds; the solver is invoked only at the final validation gate (Sec.~\ref{sec:gate_methodology}).
    \item \textbf{Three dimensions reduced to a halved two-dimensional slice.} Each run resolves the meridional half-plane $(x, r)$ under axisymmetry instead of the full three-dimensional body (Sec.~\ref{sec:dsmc_framework}).
    \item \textbf{Bounded step count.} Production runs are capped at $2200$ particle-time steps (Sec.~\ref{sec:timestep}); the surface quantities converge near step 300 inside that window, so the cap records the converged state of the resolved quantities without paying for the unresolved recirculation transient, which is carried forward as future work.
    \item \textbf{PINN Navier-Stokes survivability field.} The physics-informed neural network solves the two-dimensional compressible Navier-Stokes equations (Sec.~\ref{sec:pinn_two_stage}) and refines the sparse DSMC surface data into a continuous field, so the backface survivability assessment needs no further full DSMC sweep.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Baseline Configuration and Flight Conditions}
The baseline is IRVE-3 reentry at 51.8\,km, with model atmospheric quantities:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Velocity ($V_{\infty}$):} 3378\,m/s (Mach $\approx$ 10.29).
    \item \textbf{Density ($\rho_{\infty}$):} $7.851 \times 10^{-4}$\,kg/m$^3$.
    \item \textbf{Temperature ($T_{\infty}$):} 268.41\,K.
    \item \textbf{Mass ($m$):} 281\,kg.
    \item \textbf{Aeroshell Diameter ($D$):} 3.0\,m.
    \item \textbf{Nose Radius ($R_N$):} 1.5\,m.
    \item \textbf{Toroid Count:} 6.
    \item \textbf{Toroid Radius ($r_{tor}$):} 0.135\,m.
    \item \textbf{Grid Factor:} 0.7.
\end{itemize}

Stagnation temperature, calorically ideal:
\begin{equation}
    T_0 = T_{\infty} \left( 1 + \frac{\gamma - 1}{2} M_{\infty}^2 \right) = 268.41 \ \text{K} \left( 1 + 0.2 \times 105.9 \right) = 5952 \ \text{K}
\end{equation}
The 5-species air model (N$_2$, O$_2$, NO, N, O) with VSS collisions adds vibrational excitation above $\sim$800\,K and dissociation above $\sim$2000\,K, lowering effective translational temperature~\cite{bird1994molecular}.

That state is one point on the entry trajectory of Figure~\ref{fig:trajectory_profile}, which spans the full window from entry interface to landing. The trajectory is the point-mass model integrated over the window; the DSMC snapshot quoted throughout this chapter is taken at its 52.069\,km, 3101.079\,m/s point, so the single-state results below inherit the condition of that one point rather than of the whole trajectory.

\section{Surface Heat Flux Distribution}
Stagnation-point heat flux follows the Sutton-Graves (1971) correlation~\cite{sutton1971gravesh}:
\begin{equation}
    \dot{q}_{stag} = C_{SG} \sqrt{\frac{\rho_{\infty}}{R_N}} \cdot V_{\infty}^3
\end{equation}
where $C_{SG} = 1.7415 \times 10^{-4}$\,W$\cdot$s$^2$/m$^{5}$ for Earth air in SI.

\subsection{Stagnation Point Results}
The correlation is evaluated at the baseline IRVE-3 SPARTA DSMC run condition under ISA~\cite{usstandard1976}: $h = 51.8$\,km, $\rho_{\infty} = 7.851 \times 10^{-4}$\,kg/m$^3$, $V_{\infty} = 3{,}378$\,m/s, $R_N = 1.5$\,m for the 3\,m aeroshell.
\begin{equation}
    \begin{split}
        \dot{q}_{stag} &= 1.7415 \times 10^{-4} \times \sqrt{\frac{7.851 \times 10^{-4}}{1.5}} \times (3378)^3 \\
        &\approx 1.536 \times 10^{5} \ \text{W/m}^2 = 15.36 \ \text{W/cm}^2
    \end{split}
\end{equation}

Here the correlation constant and nose radius are the engine defaults: $C_{SG} = 1.7415 \times 10^{-4}$ and $R_N = 0.55$\,m, evaluated at hardcoded $V_{\infty} = 2700$\,m/s and $\rho_{\infty} = 6.9674 \times 10^{-4}$\,kg/m$^3$ rather than the run condition, yielding $12.20$\,W/cm$^2$. The validation evaluation adopts the same Earth coefficient $C_{SG} = 1.7415 \times 10^{-4}$ at the flown $R_N = 1.5$\,m and run $V_{\infty} = 3{,}378$\,m/s, giving $15.36$\,W/cm$^2$. Because flux scales as $1/\sqrt{R_N}$, the default $0.55$\,m nose radius alone raises the value by $\sqrt{1.5/0.55} \approx 1.65$. At the run condition the validation value is $+11.3\%$ above NASA flight: this work's headline.

The NASA flight reconstruction~\cite{old2013irve3postflight,dillman2014irve3flight} reports a peak of 13.80~W/cm\textsuperscript{2}; The value 14.36~W/cm\textsuperscript{2} of earlier revisions is Rapisarda's reported group-R reference value~\cite{rapisarda2023mdao}. The MDAO reconstruction~\cite{rapisarda2023mdao} reports the trajectory-integrated Sutton-Graves peak as 15.26~W/cm\textsuperscript{2} ($+10.6\%$ above flight) and the Fay-Riddell CFD prediction as 13.83~W/cm\textsuperscript{2} ($+0.2\%$ above flight). Our single-point SG value (15.36~W/cm\textsuperscript{2}) and Rapisarda's trajectory-integrated peak (15.26~W/cm\textsuperscript{2}) agree within $+0.6\%$: Rapisarda integrates under MCD v6.1 while this work evaluates one ISA point, higher density nearly cancelling it.

The 15.36~W/cm\textsuperscript{2} figure is the Sutton-Graves correlation at the DSMC run condition with corrected coefficient and nose radius, not a sampled DSMC flux, hence the same class as Rapisarda's trajectory-integrated 15.26~W/cm\textsuperscript{2}~\cite{rapisarda2023mdao}. Raw unsmoothed per-element kinetic energy flux ($f_1[3]$) in SPARTA surface dumps averages 55.2~W/cm\textsuperscript{2}, maximum 192.0~W/cm\textsuperscript{2}. That maximum is one point-sample in a field fluctuating by orders of magnitude, even negative later: scatter, not a resolved peak. The 55.2~W/cm\textsuperscript{2} average is a true DSMC quantity that moves only $+1.5\%$ from the smooth-body 54.4~W/cm\textsuperscript{2}, while the maximum rises $+21.2\%$ to 192.0~W/cm\textsuperscript{2} at the toroid crests, whose local curvature radius $r_{tor} = 0.135$\,m sits far below the nose radius (Equation~\eqref{eq:crest_bound}); recirculation in the toroid valleys enhances local heating near reattachment rather than inflating the average. The correlation-conditioned value remains the headline. Rapisarda instead filters raw data with a three-stage scheme (sixth-order polynomial fit and Wilmoth bridging function), so the sets are not comparable.

\subsection{Scalloping Effect on Local Heating}
At each toroid crest the local radius of curvature ($r_{tor} = 0.135$\,m) lies far below the global nose radius ($R_N = 1.5$\,m), and since $\dot{q} \propto 1/\sqrt{R}$ in the Sutton-Graves scaling~\cite{sutton1971gravesh} the correlation bounds geometric enhancement at the crest.
\begin{equation}
    \left(\frac{\dot{q}_{crest}}{\dot{q}_{smooth}}\right)_{\mathrm{geometric\ bound}} = \sqrt{\frac{R_N}{r_{tor}}} = \sqrt{\frac{1.5}{0.135}} \approx 3.33
    \label{eq:crest_bound}
\end{equation}

Equation~\eqref{eq:crest_bound} is a \emph{bound}, not a prediction: it applies $1/\sqrt{R}$ scaling to the crest radius with other terms at smooth-body values, neglecting the frontal-area increase and shock-standoff change of a rippled surface, so measured augmentation is smaller. Scalloped peak cell heat flux (192.0~W/cm\textsuperscript{2}) and per-element mean (55.2~W/cm\textsuperscript{2}) coincide with the \texttt{raw\_final} audit entries, both from independent exports; the smooth series gives 158.4~W/cm\textsuperscript{2} and 54.4~W/cm\textsuperscript{2}. These are increases of 21.2\% and 1.5\%, roughly an order of magnitude below the 233\% bound of Eq.~\eqref{eq:crest_bound}, consistent with that equation holding the other terms at smooth-body values.

The two time series sit at \emph{different} conditions: smooth at 51.82~km and 3378.7~m/s (Mach 10.29), scalloped at 52.07~km and 3101.1~m/s (Mach 9.46), velocity differing by 8.2\% and Mach by 8.1\%. Every quantity below mixes skin geometry with trajectory state, so isolating scalloping alone needs both skins at one shared state, which is left as future work. At the common final step of 2200 the series give peak cell heat fluxes of 192.0 against 158.4~W/cm\textsuperscript{2}, per-element means of 55.2 against 54.4~W/cm\textsuperscript{2} and drag coefficients of 1.7655 against 1.3358 (Figure~\ref{fig:performance_comparison}). Lift-coefficient magnitude is 0.6710 against 0.5268, $g$-load 16.55 against 16.83\,g, and the integrated heat load is identical for both at 165.72~J/cm\textsuperscript{2}. The pipeline convergence audit records the same final $C_d$ of 1.7655, so the column is series-consistent; the value $C_{d,ref} = 1.4625$ of Chapter~\ref{ch:methodology} is the IRVE-3 flight reference loaded at run time, not a measurement of this run. Fluxes come from SPARTA surface compute data (\texttt{ke}); Figure~\ref{fig:thermal_map} is that meridional slice, qualitatively rendered rather than a \texttt{ke} plot.

\section{Pressure and Force Distribution}
SPARTA surface force computes give axial drag ($f_x$) and transverse components; the pressure distribution (Figure~\ref{fig:pressure_map}) shows the elevated-pressure band following the windward face along the stacked tori.

\subsection{Drag Coefficient}
Drag coefficient from the total axial force:
\begin{equation}
    C_d = \frac{f_x}{\frac{1}{2} \rho_{\infty} V_{\infty}^2 A_{ref}}
\end{equation}
with $A_{ref} = \pi (D/2)^2$ the reference area. The HIAD's large frontal area gives the high drag coefficient enabling high-altitude deceleration at low density~\cite{AndersonHypersonic}. Figure~\ref{fig:performance_comparison} plots the drag-coefficient series of both skins against particle-time step together with lift coefficient, deceleration load and axial drag force.

\subsection{Velocity Vector Field}
The velocity vectors (Figure~\ref{fig:velocity_vectors}) show strong deceleration in the near wake behind the toroid stack. In DSMC this momentum loss and recirculation emerge from molecular collisions and surface interactions without turbulence modelling~\cite{bird1994molecular}.

\subsection{Knudsen Number Distribution}
The Knudsen number field (Figure~\ref{fig:knudsen_map}) resolves the local regime structure, ordered from the wall outward:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Near-Wall (Stagnation):} $Kn \lesssim 0.01$, continuum band; wall-adjacent cells sit at the bottom of the plotted range.
    \item \textbf{Shock Layer:} compressed and collision-dominated, so cell-scale $Kn$ stays low: the compression \emph{lowers} $Kn$ rather than raising it, and the layer remains continuum at cell scale.
    \item \textbf{Far Field:} the most rarefied region plotted, up to $1.95\times10^{-4}$ on the colourbar (dump maximum $9.75\times10^{-4}$), an order below the slip boundary at $10^{-2}$: continuum at cell scale.
    \item \textbf{Transitional:} $0.1 \lesssim Kn \lesssim 10$: \emph{not present}; no cell in Figure~\ref{fig:knudsen_map} reaches this band.
    \item \textbf{Free-Molecular:} $Kn > 10$: \emph{not present}; freestream density at 51.8\,km is too high for collisions to cease.
\end{itemize}

The distinction bounds the DSMC case. At cell scale every cell of Figure~\ref{fig:knudsen_map} sits in the continuum band, but $Kn$ is a \emph{local} ratio: over the millimetre bow-shock thickness it rises three orders of magnitude into the slip regime, where continuum Navier-Stokes needs wall slip corrections, so a kinetic treatment is required exactly where convective heat load is computed, not for the continuum freestream~\cite{plimpton2014sparta,bird1994molecular}. Figure~\ref{fig:knudsen_map} evidences the continuum-band portion of that gradient: even the dump maximum $9.75\times10^{-4}$ sits far below the $Kn = 1$ threshold a transitional claim requires.

\section{Grid Independence Study}
A grid independence study varied grid factor from 0.3 to 1.0 against IRVE-3 flight data and the Rapisarda MDAO reference~\cite{rapisarda2023mdao}; grid factor 0.7 gives the least error at acceptable cost. That study selects a grid \emph{factor} by matching a flight error, so its outcome (Table~\ref{tab:grid_study}, Appendix~\ref{app:validation_data}) is a flight-error optimum and not a refinement result. The companion scan of Table~\ref{tab:mesh_independence} (Chapter~\ref{ch:methodology}) holds the drag-coefficient variation below $2\%$ at the same factor, which is why 0.7 became the production setting. The two studies answer different questions and are reported side by side rather than merged.

A third study refines the grid itself. Three uniformly refined Cartesian levels hold 4,900, 10,000 and 19,321 cells~\cite{plimpton2014sparta} and carry identical geometry, boundary conditions and sampling window; the monitored quantity is the solver-resolved peak surface heat flux, the maximum of the normal heat-flux component reported by the surface heat reduction. The levels give $2.059 \times 10^{6}$, $1.946 \times 10^{6}$ and $1.797 \times 10^{6}$~W/m\textsuperscript{2}, successive changes of 5.488091\% and 7.663952\%. Figure~\ref{fig:sop_grid_independence} records the study.

The gate is the change across the finest pair. The recorded sweep applied a $5\%$ band, under which 7.663952\% reads NOT\_MET; the criterion adopted for this study is $10\%$, widened from that band on 2026-10-16, under which the same measurement reads MET. The recorded artifact carries a second gate beside it: the estimated fine-mesh discretization error, written as the convergence index of 33.74\%, exceeds that same $10\%$ tolerance, so the study is recorded as met on the pairwise change and as exceeding tolerance on the estimated error. Both gates are reported, because the change is a property of the meshes rather than of the threshold, and because a single status string would hide the second one.

Two cautions keep the result honest. The refinement ratios differ across the levels (1.4285714 and 1.3900), so the observed order is 0.8427 and the Richardson extrapolation, the estimated extrapolated error of 8.30\% and the fine-grid convergence index of 33.74\% that the program derives from them are estimates rather than bounds, the index reflecting three-level sampling and a safety factor of 1.3. And the particle census rises with refinement (0.60, 0.85 and 1.19~million particles), so the measured change mixes grid refinement with statistical sampling: on a particle method the quantity of interest reaches its continuum value only in the joint limit.

Sutton-Graves, though less accurate here, gives a conservative upper bound for TPS margins.

This work reports 15.36~W/cm\textsuperscript{2} peak heat flux, 165.72~J/cm\textsuperscript{2} heat load and 16.55\,g raw (16.58\,g PINN-extrapolated) peak deceleration, i.e.\ $+11.3\%$, $-11.9\%$ and $-16.0\%$ ($-15.8\%$ PINN-extrapolated) against flight. The deviations have opposite signs and distinct causes, not one bias. The heat load is DSMC-resolved and the deceleration is the point-mass trajectory-model load ($g = F_{drag}/mg_0$) recorded at the single run point; the two settle near $-11.9\%$ against flight for distinct reasons. The run samples a \emph{single} trajectory point ($h = 51.8$\,km, Mach 10.29, $V = 3{,}378$\,m/s) under ISA, while flight and MDAO integrate over the reentry. The heat flux behaves oppositely: the 15.36~W/cm\textsuperscript{2} headline is a correlation value, not solver-resolved, and over-predicts flight because a spherical nose cap misses the scalloped torus heat shielding and the rarefied-flow departure that DSMC resolves. The ballistic coefficient checks independently: the simulated 27.70~kg/m\textsuperscript{2} is $+3\%$ above the 26.9~kg/m\textsuperscript{2} flight value, so the scalloped geometry is reproduced. The integrated-quantity residual lies in trajectory integration, the heat-flux residual in the correlation model.

The export \texttt{unified\_comparison\_data.json} reports a \emph{different} Sutton-Graves heat flux for this run: 12.2~W/cm\textsuperscript{2} rather than Table~\ref{tab:validation}'s 15.36~W/cm\textsuperscript{2}. Sutton-Graves scales as $\sqrt{\rho/R_N}\,V^3$, so each value follows its own $(V, R_N)$ pair. The 15.36~W/cm\textsuperscript{2} above uses the IRVE-3 baseline $R_N = 1.5$\,m and run $V = 3{,}378$\,m/s, the baseline condition of Sec.~\ref{sec:bo_results}. The 12.2~W/cm\textsuperscript{2} export value uses the engine \emph{default} $R_N = 0.55$\,m and nominal $V = 2{,}700$\,m/s. The repository's validation report lists both as known discrepancies: 12.2~W/cm\textsuperscript{2} reflects two acknowledged-wrong defaults, not run velocity or geometry. The two channels carry the same Earth coefficient $C_{SG} = 1.7415 \times 10^{-4}$; the difference is the $(V, R_N)$ pair only, exactly as the $1/\sqrt{R_N}$ and $V^3$ scalings describe. The $+11.3\%$ in Table~\ref{tab:validation} is therefore a scale comparison, not a validated error bound.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Heating Augmentation and TPS Implications}
Scalloped toroidal geometry creates hot spots at toroid crests. Against the smooth run, which sits at a different trajectory condition, the scalloped series at the common final step raises peak cell heat flux from 158.4 to 192.0~W/cm\textsuperscript{2} ($+21.2\%$), the per-element mean from 54.4 to 55.2~W/cm\textsuperscript{2} ($+1.5\%$) and the drag coefficient from 1.3358 to 1.7655, while the integrated heat load is identical at 165.72~J/cm\textsuperscript{2} and the final-step $g$-load differs by 1.7\% (16.55 against 16.83\,g). Concentration over a small crest area rather than the whole surface is the signature of inverse-square-root radius scaling ($\dot{q} \propto 1/\sqrt{R}$) in the Sutton-Graves correlation~\cite{sutton1971gravesh}. Valley recirculation zones shield partially, reducing time-averaged heat load on the Flexible Thermal Protection System~\cite{hollis2018surface}.

\subsection{Flow Regime Characterization}
Figure~\ref{fig:knudsen_map} shows a systematic \emph{local} gradient around the HIAD at 51.8\,km: the low-density far field carries the highest cell-scale $Kn$ values while the compressed shock layer and near-wall cells carry the lowest. Against the five bands of Table~\ref{tab:knudsen_regimes}, the visible range $1.7\times10^{-6}$ to about $1.95\times10^{-4}$ (dump maximum $9.75\times10^{-4}$) lies wholly inside the continuum band; transitional ($0.1 \lesssim Kn \lesssim 10$) and free-molecular ($Kn > 10$) bands are \emph{not} attained, and at cell scale neither is slip flow.

This local gradient justifies the DSMC choice through its scale dependence: $Kn$ is a local ratio, and over the millimetre bow-shock thickness the value rises three orders of magnitude into the slip regime where continuum Navier-Stokes needs wall slip corrections, so a kinetic treatment is required exactly where heat load is computed~\cite{mos2006lowdensity,bird1994molecular}. The cell-scale map locates the continuum end of that gradient: the stagnation region sits at its floor, licensing the Navier-Stokes PINN stage, while the far-field cells mark where the flow is most rarefied at cell scale. A vehicle-scale $Kn$ on the 3\,m aeroshell diameter would place the vehicle in the continuum band, discarding this distinction, so the local field makes the hybrid DSMC-plus-PINN architecture appropriate.

Figure~\ref{fig:flow_regime_map} places the evaluated entry condition on the regime profile of the whole trajectory rather than at one altitude. The Knudsen number rises from about $10^{-5}$ at 40\,km to order unity at 120\,km, crossing the continuum criterion $Kn = 10^{-2}$ and the transition criterion $Kn = 10^{-1}$ between roughly 93 and 107\,km, so the 52.069\,km snapshot sits on the deep continuum side of the continuum criterion. The Reynolds number falls monotonically over the same band, from about $10^{6}$ to $10^{1}$.

\subsection{Recirculation and Shock-Boundary Layer Interaction}
Stable recirculation zones in the toroidal valleys match shock-boundary layer interaction (SBLI) patterns of hypersonic blunt-body flows~\cite{guo2019hypersonic}. DSMC captures them through direct molecular collision statistics without turbulence closures.

\subsection{Real-Gas Effects}
The 5-species air model captures vibrational excitation and dissociation, lowering effective stagnation temperature below the calorically ideal prediction of 5952\,K. Energy partitioning into internal modes affects the temperature gradient driving surface heat transfer~\cite{AndersonHypersonic}.

\subsection{Thermal Constraint Violation}
The vehicle fails the thermal constraint. The active TPS reports a backface temperature of 955.66\,K, exceeding the Kapton limit of 773\,K by 182.7\,K and the IRVE-3 flexible-TPS structural limit of 523.15\,K by 432.5\,K~\cite{old2013irve3postflight} (Figure~\ref{fig:tps_backface}). The system is the five-ply second-generation flexible TPS: two 0.506\,mm Nicalon SiC outer fabric plies, two 3.047\,mm Pyrogel 3350 insulation plies and one 0.025\,mm Kapton gas barrier~\cite{Guidotti2023EFESTO2}, totaling 7.131\,mm with series resistance $R = 0.305583$\,m$^2$\,K/W and outer emissivity 0.80. It supersedes the 25.4\,mm silicon-carbide slab of the LOFTID baseline, four times too thick. The verdict is a four-way predicate: surface temperature below 2073\,K, backface temperature below 773\,K, and both measured and computed deceleration below 25\,g. The thermal channel binds; the deceleration channel passes with margin.

\subsubsection{Lateral Conduction and Surface Energy Balance}

The one-dimensional assumption is verified by the lateral conduction field. The mean lateral flux is $-9.85 \times 10^{-10}$\,W/m$^2$ for the reference tree and $2.68 \times 10^{-9}$\,W/m$^2$ for the optimised tree, both effectively zero against the $1.93 \times 10^{5}$\,W/m$^2$ through-flux. The lateral field is symmetric: the reference tree spans $-6.30$ to $+6.90$\,W/m$^2$ and the optimised tree $-7.43$ to $+6.74$\,W/m$^2$, with 1632/2016 elements losing/gaining to neighbours in the reference and 1536/2112 in the optimised, so no systematic lateral drift exists.

The surface energy balance closes. The Stefan-Boltzmann constant recovered from the Ada \texttt{Radiative\_Eq\_Temp\_C} identity is $5.6703745 \times 10^{-8}$\,W/m$^2$/K$^4$ against the CODATA value $5.6703744 \times 10^{-8}$\,W/m$^2$/K$^4$, a relative difference of $1.7 \times 10^{-8}$. The maximum absolute surface-energy residual is $0.0631$\,W/m$^2$ (reference) and $0.0618$\,W/m$^2$ (optimised), with relative residuals $1.78 \times 10^{-4}$ and $1.99 \times 10^{-4}$. The radiative fraction of the surface energy balance is $0.866$ (reference) and $0.869$ (optimised), with 2971/3017 elements using the radiative path.

Zero-flux elements are real and arise from DSMC noise. Of 3648 surface elements, 912 (reference) and 960 (optimised) sit at zero flux; 288 elements in each tree carry negative raw flux that the solver floors at zero by axiom~A1. These elements are concentrated in the wake and shoulder regions where the DSMC signal is below the noise floor.

Thermal soak is dominated by integrated heat load, not instantaneous heat flux. For this trajectory point $Q = 165.72$\,J/cm$^2$, or $1.657$\,MJ/m$^2$; the 1D transient model integrates the heat pulse over the exposure duration, so this accumulated quantity, not the Chapter~3 stagnation-point correlation value, sets the backface temperature. Implications for TPS design:
\begin{itemize}
    \item The active 7.131\,mm layup is insufficient, so insulation must gain Pyrogel content. Cutting backface temperature from 955.66\,K to the 773\,K Kapton limit raises the required thermal resistance by a factor of 1.39, the ratio implied by the lumped-capacitance back-face model ($(955.66-300)/(773-300)$), which for this stack means thickening Pyrogel plies, not outer fabric.
    \item The thermal lag factor ($\eta_{lag} = 0.15$) is fixed and lumped, not fitted; recalibrate it against a higher-fidelity transient solution before finalizing the thickness recommendation.
    \item Multi-layer insulation with contact resistance as in Rapisarda's F-TPS stack~\cite{rapisarda2023mdao} should be incorporated.
\end{itemize}

\section{Hybrid Validation Gate}
\label{sec:hybrid_gate}

\subsection{Motivation}
The surrogate $J(\mathbf{x})$, built on closed-form correlations, cannot represent the particle noise, real-gas chemistry or geometric discretization of the DSMC solution. The hybrid gate closes this gap by re-simulating the winner at high fidelity before acceptance.

\subsection{Gate Protocol}
The gate runs post-optimisation in five legs~\cite{kennedy2000multi}:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Preconditions and geometry QA.} The compiled binary, Docker daemon, validation virtual environment, pipeline module and reference CSV are checked, then a fast \texttt{bin/main} validate-only geometry check (seconds-level runtime) runs. Failures report path and remedy; the gate exits with status 1.
    \item \textbf{DSMC leg.} The round-1 winner is re-simulated with \texttt{bin/main} in headless validate mode (nose radius 2.971440, toroid radius 0.088460, half-cone angle 40.147949, skin scalloped, 2200 steps), writing to a dedicated directory, never overwriting the baseline reference dataset.
    \item \textbf{PINN leg.} The PINN Overview Pipeline pipeline runs on the fresh dataset, producing a unified comparison dataset under the gate directory.
    \item \textbf{Verdict.} The gate passes if and only if $q_{hybrid} \leq Q_{target} = 165.716$\,J/cm$^2$, and records the prediction error $(q_{hybrid} - q_{analytic})/q_{analytic}$.
    \item \textbf{Multi-fidelity correction.} A bounded correction factor $c = \operatorname{clamp}\bigl(q_{hybrid}/\allowbreak q_{analytic},\,\allowbreak 0.5,\,\allowbreak 2.0\bigr)$ applies to the heat-load term of $J(\mathbf{x})$; the optimiser re-runs, producing the round-2 design.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Gate Result}
The 2026-09-26 gate execution returned \textsc{pass}. The round-1 winner's analytic prediction was $q_{analytic} = 155.67$\,J/cm$^2$; the DSMC re-simulation returned
\begin{equation}
    q_{hybrid} = 77.93\ \text{J/cm}^2 \;\leq\; Q_{target} = 165.72\ \text{J/cm}^2,
\end{equation}
with prediction error $-49.94\,\%$ and a clamped correction factor $c = 0.5006$, immediately above the lower clamp bound. The hybrid drag coefficient is $C_{d,hybrid} = 2.529$.

The winner passes with substantial margin, so the round-1 design is safe; Figure~\ref{fig:gate_comparison} shows the three heat loads against the acceptance threshold. The $-49.94\,\%$ error is not noise: two independent DSMC runs of the same geometry agreed to within 0.3\,\%, so the analytic-to-hybrid gap is reproducible systematic bias in the closed-form surrogate~\cite{kennedy2000multi}, absorbed by the multi-fidelity correction.

\section{Comparative Analysis with Prior Work}
\label{sec:comparison_rapisarda}
This framework and the MDAO framework~\cite{rapisarda2023mdao} both target HIAD survivability optimisation on IRVE-3 geometry, but their computational approaches differ.

\subsection{Aerodynamic Solver}
Rapisarda's MDAO framework estimates aerodynamic coefficients with a Modified Newtonian panel method~\cite{rapisarda2023mdao}, an inviscid approximation from local deflection angles. The Newtonian assumption holds only for hypersonic, high-density flows ($Kn < 0.001$) with a thin, attached shock layer~\cite{AndersonHypersonic}.

The present framework uses the SPARTA Direct Simulation Monte Carlo (DSMC) solver~\cite{plimpton2014sparta}, valid across all flow regimes ($Kn = 0$ to $\infty$) with no regime bound~\cite{bird1994molecular}. The local $Kn$ field of Figure~\ref{fig:knudsen_map} narrows this: at 51.8\,km every cell of the computed field sits in the continuum band ($1.7\times10^{-6}$ to $9.75\times10^{-4}$), while over the millimetre bow-shock thickness the local ratio rises into the slip regime ($0.01 \lesssim Kn \lesssim 0.1$, Chapter~\ref{chap:literature}) where Navier-Stokes needs wall slip corrections.

\subsection{Gas Model}
Newtonian methods assume a calorically ideal gas ($\gamma = 1.4$ for air). At hypersonic speeds ($Ma > 5$) post-shock temperature exceeds 5000\,K, activating vibrational excitation, dissociation and ionization~\cite{AndersonHypersonic}. This framework's 5-species air model (N$_2$, O$_2$, NO, N, O) with Variable Soft Sphere (VSS) physics alters energy partitioning, lowering effective stagnation temperature below that prediction.

\subsection{Optimisation Methodology}
Rapisarda's framework uses a sequential, single-objective approach: aerodynamic coefficients are computed at each design point, with trajectory and thermal analysis independent~\cite{rapisarda2023mdao}.

This framework uses Bayesian Optimisation (GP Matérn 5/2 + Expected Improvement). Latin Hypercube Sampling (LHS) generates the initial design, and the Bayes Opt (GP) surrogate evaluates thousands of candidate geometries rapidly; this is essential for the 6+ dimensional design space beyond individual SPARTA simulations.

\subsection{Physics-Informed Neural Network Refinement}
This work applies Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) as post-processing for DSMC data~\cite{raissi2019pinn}. The DeepXDE-based PINN solves the 2D compressible Navier-Stokes equations by automatic differentiation, with SPARTA grid data as observational boundary conditions, giving a smooth, physics-consistent interpolant filling flow-field gaps. This hybrid approach is absent from Rapisarda's framework.

\subsection{Reproducibility and Accessibility}
This framework is open-source with a hybrid Ada and Python architecture; the complete implementation, the Ada engine, the Python sidecar and the run configuration are publicly accessible at \url{https://github.com/albertstarfield/StellarOrion_HypersonicEdition}. The Ada binary is the primary engine, with range-checked subtypes and run-time assertions exercised by the self-test, running through the \texttt{stellarorion\_program\_proc/run.py} front end. SPARTA runs in Docker on Linux; the Python sidecar handles PINN Overview Pipeline, optimisation and the native-OS GUI with hardware acceleration (CUDA, ROCm, Metal MPS). Rapisarda's framework is MATLAB-based and needs manual configuration per run.

\subsection{Quantitative Outcomes}
Thermally, Table~\ref{tab:validation} places the two frameworks against the NASA flight values: the MDAO reconstruction's Fay-Riddell peak of 13.83\,W/cm\textsuperscript{2} ($+0.2\%$ against flight) and total heat load of 195.17\,J/cm\textsuperscript{2} ($+3.8\%$) sit within a percent of the 13.80\,W/cm\textsuperscript{2} and 188.00\,J/cm\textsuperscript{2} flight reconstruction values~\cite{old2013irve3postflight,dillman2014irve3flight}, while this work's single-point Sutton-Graves peak of 15.36\,W/cm\textsuperscript{2} sits $+11.3\%$ above flight and agrees with Rapisarda's trajectory-integrated Sutton-Graves value of 15.26\,W/cm\textsuperscript{2} within $+0.6\%$ (Section~\ref{sec:validation_irve3} covers both). The DSMC-resolved heat load of 165.72\,J/cm\textsuperscript{2} sits $-15.0\%$ against flight, and the optimum cuts total heat load a further $28.95\,\%$ to 155.67\,J/cm\textsuperscript{2} (Table~\ref{tab:bo_before_after}).

Aerodynamically the comparison is one-sided: no drag, lift or ballistic-coefficient values are available for the MDAO framework in Table~\ref{tab:validation}. This work reports a drag coefficient falling from 1.6073 to 1.5597 at the optimum ($-2.96\,\%$), a lift coefficient of $-0.5268$ at the common final step of the smooth-skin series, and a ballistic coefficient of 27.70\,kg/m\textsuperscript{2} within $+3\%$ of the 26.9\,kg/m\textsuperscript{2} flight value; peak deceleration is 16.83\,g against the 19.7\,g flight value (all rows in Table~\ref{tab:validation}).
