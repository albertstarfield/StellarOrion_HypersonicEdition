\chapter{Results and Discussion}
\label{ch:results}

\section{Overview}
This chapter presents the simulation results and discussion derived from the SPARTA DSMC computational framework described in Chapter~\ref{ch:methodology}. The analysis covers the baseline IRVE-3 geometry configuration, the aerothermal surface distributions, flow field characteristics, and the validation against flight data. All results are generated using the StellarOrion engine with the 6-toroid stacked HIAD geometry at the MDAO baseline parameters~\cite{rapisarda2023mdao}.

\section{Baseline Configuration and Flight Conditions}
The baseline simulation corresponds to the IRVE-3 reentry trajectory point at 51.8\,km altitude, with the following freestream conditions. The atmospheric quantities are the values returned by the atmospheric model evaluated at that altitude:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Freestream Velocity ($V_{\infty}$):} 3378\,m/s (Mach $\approx$ 10.29)
    \item \textbf{Freestream Density ($\rho_{\infty}$):} $7.851 \times 10^{-4}$\,kg/m$^3$ (atmospheric model, code baseline)
    \item \textbf{Freestream Temperature ($T_{\infty}$):} 268.41\,K (atmospheric model, code baseline)
    \item \textbf{Vehicle Mass ($m$):} 281\,kg
    \item \textbf{Aeroshell Diameter ($D$):} 3.0\,m
    \item \textbf{Nose Radius ($R_N$):} 1.5\,m
    \item \textbf{Toroid Count:} 6 (stacked toroid configuration)
    \item \textbf{Toroid Radius ($r_{tor}$):} 0.135\,m
    \item \textbf{Grid Factor:} 0.7 (validated optimal against IRVE-3 data)
\end{itemize}

The stagnation temperature for a calorically perfect gas at these conditions is:
\begin{equation}
    T_0 = T_{\infty} \left( 1 + \frac{\gamma - 1}{2} M_{\infty}^2 \right) = 268.41 \ \text{K} \left( 1 + 0.2 \times 105.9 \right) = 5952 \ \text{K}
\end{equation}
However, the 5-species air model (N$_2$, O$_2$, NO, N, O) with VSS collisions captures real-gas effects including vibrational excitation above $\sim$800\,K and dissociation above $\sim$2000\,K, resulting in a lower effective translational temperature~\cite{bird1994molecular}.

\section{Surface Heat Flux Distribution}
The stagnation-point heat flux is computed using the Sutton--Graves (1971) correlation~\cite{sutton1971gravesh}:
\begin{equation}
    \dot{q}_{stag} = C_{SG} \sqrt{\frac{\rho_{\infty}}{R_N}} \cdot V_{\infty}^3
\end{equation}
where $C_{SG} = 1.83 \times 10^{-4}$\,W$\cdot$s$^2$/m$^{5}$ for Earth air in SI units.

\subsection{Stagnation Point Results}
For the baseline IRVE-3 configuration the correlation is evaluated at the SPARTA DSMC run condition under ISA~\cite{usstandard1976}: $h = 51.8$\,km, $\rho_{\infty} = 7.851 \times 10^{-4}$\,kg/m$^3$, $V_{\infty} = 3{,}378$\,m/s, with the IRVE-3 nose radius $R_N = 1.5$\,m corresponding to the 3\,m aeroshell diameter:
\begin{equation}
    \begin{split}
        \dot{q}_{stag} &= 1.83 \times 10^{-4} \times \sqrt{\frac{7.851 \times 10^{-4}}{1.5}} \times (3378)^3 \\
        &\approx 1.614 \times 10^{5} \ \text{W/m}^2 = 16.14 \ \text{W/cm}^2
    \end{split}
\end{equation}

The correlation constant and the nose radius used here both differ from the engine defaults, so their provenance is stated. The engine default is $C_{SG} = 1.7415 \times 10^{-4}$ and $R_N = 0.55$\,m; the pair evaluated at a hardcoded $V_{\infty} = 2700$\,m/s and $\rho_{\infty} = 6.9674 \times 10^{-4}$\,kg/m$^3$ rather than at the run condition, and yields $12.20$\,W/cm$^2$. Because the correlation scales as $1/\sqrt{R_N}$, the engine-default $0.55$\,m nose radius alone raises the computed flux by a factor of $\sqrt{1.5/0.55} \approx 1.65$. At the run condition the value is $+12.4\%$ relative to the flight measurement, and it is this work's headline stagnation heat flux.

The IRVE-3 flight measurement reports a peak heat flux of 14.36~W/cm\textsuperscript{2}~\cite{old2013irve3postflight}. The MDAO reconstruction~\cite{rapisarda2023mdao} reports the trajectory-integrated Sutton--Graves peak as 15.26~W/cm\textsuperscript{2} ($+6.26\%$ above flight) and the Fay--Riddell CFD prediction as 13.83~W/cm\textsuperscript{2} ($-3.69\%$ below flight). The Fay--Riddell correlation is more physically accurate for HIAD geometries because it accounts for the actual shock layer structure, while Sutton--Graves assumes a spherical nose cap. Our single-point SG value (16.14~W/cm\textsuperscript{2}) and Rapisarda's trajectory-integrated peak (15.26~W/cm\textsuperscript{2}) agree to within $+5.8\%$ despite the different atmospheric density profiles: Rapisarda integrates a parameterized trajectory under MCD v6.1 while our SPARTA baseline evaluates a single ISA point, and the higher density of the former very nearly cancels the trajectory integration of the latter.

It is important to state precisely what the 16.14~W/cm\textsuperscript{2} figure represents, because it is \emph{not} a directly sampled DSMC heat flux. It is the Sutton--Graves correlation evaluated at the DSMC run condition using the corrected coefficient and nose radius; it is therefore a DSMC-conditioned SG prediction rather than a raw DSMC measurement, and it is the same class of quantity as Rapisarda's trajectory-integrated SG value of 15.26~W/cm\textsuperscript{2}~\cite{rapisarda2023mdao}. The raw DSMC surface data tell a different and noisier story. Reading the unsmoothed per-element kinetic energy flux ($f_1[3]$) from the SPARTA surface dumps yields a per-element average of 56.6~W/cm\textsuperscript{2} and a maximum single-cell sample of 182.5~W/cm\textsuperscript{2}. The maximum is a single point-sample in a field where individual cells fluctuate by orders of magnitude, including negative values at later time steps, and is statistical scatter rather than a resolved physical peak. The per-element average of 56.6~W/cm\textsuperscript{2} is a true DSMC quantity but is inflated relative to the stagnation-point correlation because it averages over the whole scalloped surface, including the low-heating recirculation pockets, and because it is taken from an unsmoothed noisy field. For this reason the correlation-conditioned value is used as the headline heat-flux metric throughout, and the raw DSMC numbers are reported here only to document the noise characteristics of the surface data. Rapisarda avoids this ambiguity by applying a three-stage filter (a sixth-order polynomial fit followed by a Wilmoth bridging function) to the same kind of raw DSMC data, which is another reason the two heat-flux sets are not directly comparable.

\subsection{Scalloping Effect on Local Heating}
The toroidal surface topology introduces localized variations in the radius of curvature. At each toroid crest, the local radius of curvature ($r_{tor} = 0.135$\,m) is much smaller than the global nose radius ($R_N = 1.5$\,m). Since $\dot{q} \propto 1/\sqrt{R}$ in the Sutton--Graves scaling~\cite{sutton1971gravesh}, the correlation sets an upper bound on the geometric enhancement available at a crest:
\begin{equation}
    \left(\frac{\dot{q}_{crest}}{\dot{q}_{smooth}}\right)_{\mathrm{geometric\ bound}} = \sqrt{\frac{R_N}{r_{tor}}} = \sqrt{\frac{1.5}{0.135}} \approx 3.33
    \label{eq:crest_bound}
\end{equation}

Equation~\eqref{eq:crest_bound} is a \emph{bound} and not a prediction: it applies the $1/\sqrt{R}$ scaling to the crest radius while holding every other term of the correlation at its smooth-body value, and it neglects the concurrent increase in frontal area and the change in effective shock standoff that accompany a rippled surface. The measured augmentation is therefore substantially smaller than this bound. The augmentation reported here is read from the two recorded time series at their common final step of 2200. The scalloped figures, a peak cell heat flux of 182.5~W/cm\textsuperscript{2} and a per-element mean of 56.6~W/cm\textsuperscript{2}, coincide with the \texttt{raw\_final} entries of the scalloped run's separate convergence audit, so those two quantities rest on two independent exports; the smooth figures, 158.4~W/cm\textsuperscript{2} and 54.4~W/cm\textsuperscript{2}, come from the smooth run's own series. These are increases of 15.2\% and 4.0\% on the flux quantities: consistent in sign and in ordering with the geometric bound of Eq.~\eqref{eq:crest_bound}, and roughly a factor of five below it.

This comparison is not, however, a controlled one, and the limitation must be stated rather than glossed. The two recorded time series are held at \emph{different} flight conditions: the smooth series at 51.82~km and 3378.7~m/s (Mach 10.29) and the scalloped series at 52.03~km and 3094.2~m/s (Mach 9.43), a freestream velocity difference of 8.4\% and a Mach difference of 8.3\%. Every quantity derived from those two runs is therefore a joint effect of skin geometry and trajectory state, and none of the differences below can be attributed to scalloping alone. Read at the common final step of 2200, the two series give a peak cell heat flux of 182.5 against 158.4~W/cm\textsuperscript{2}, a per-element mean of 56.6 against 54.4~W/cm\textsuperscript{2}, a drag coefficient of 1.7579 against 1.3358, a lift-coefficient magnitude of 0.6683 against 0.5268, a peak $g$-load of 14.01 against 16.83\,g, and an integrated heat load of 174.14 against 165.72~J/cm\textsuperscript{2}. The two heat-flux quantities are independently corroborated, since they coincide with the final entries of the scalloped run's separate convergence audit to four significant figures. The drag coefficient is not: that audit records $C_d = 1.4625$ where the time series records 1.75791, so its drag column appears to hold a fitted or aggregated value rather than the final sampled one, and the discrepancy is disclosed here rather than resolved by selection. Isolating the scalloping effect from the trajectory difference requires both skins to be run at one shared state, which is identified as future work in Sec.~\ref{sec:optimization_results}. Those scalar kinetic energy fluxes are read from the SPARTA surface compute data (\texttt{ke}); Figure~\ref{fig:thermal_map} shows the temperature field of the same meridional slice and is a qualitative rendering, not a plot of \texttt{ke}.

\subsection{Drag Coefficient}
The drag coefficient is computed from the total axial force:
\begin{equation}
    C_d = \frac{f_x}{\frac{1}{2} \rho_{\infty} V_{\infty}^2 A_{ref}}
\end{equation}
where $A_{ref} = \pi (D/2)^2$ is the reference area based on the aeroshell diameter. The HIAD's large frontal area produces a high drag coefficient, which is the fundamental mechanism enabling deceleration at high altitudes where atmospheric density is low~\cite{AndersonHypersonic}.

\subsection{Knudsen Number Distribution}
The Knudsen number field (Figure~\ref{fig:knudsen_map}) resolves the local regime structure across the computational domain. Ordered from the wall outwards, the observed bands are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Near-Wall (Stagnation):} $Kn \lesssim 0.01$, continuum band, where Navier--Stokes with viscosity and heat conduction is valid
    \item \textbf{Shock Layer:} $0.01 \lesssim Kn \lesssim 0.1$, slip-flow band, valid only with velocity and temperature slip corrections at the wall, which is what motivates the kinetic solver
    \item \textbf{Transitional:} $0.1 \lesssim Kn \lesssim 10$: \emph{not present} at this trajectory point; no cell in Figure~\ref{fig:knudsen_map} reaches this band
    \item \textbf{Free-Molecular:} $Kn > 10$: \emph{not present} at this trajectory point; the freestream density at 51.8\,km is far too high for intermolecular collisions to cease
\end{itemize}

The distinction matters because it bounds the DSMC justification precisely. Since the highest-$Kn$ cells coincide with the shock layer and near-wall regions, a kinetic treatment is required exactly where the convective heat load is computed, and is not required for the continuum freestream~\cite{plimpton2014sparta}. Figure~\ref{fig:knudsen_map} must therefore be cited as evidence of a continuum-to-slip gradient, and never as evidence of transitional or free-molecular flow; the freestream $Kn$ at the $2700$\,m/s condition of that panel is far below the $Kn = 1$ threshold that such a claim would require.

The MDAO Fay--Riddell prediction of 13.83~W/cm\textsuperscript{2} is within 3.7\% of the 14.36~W/cm\textsuperscript{2} flight measurement, and its total heat load of 195.17~J/cm\textsuperscript{2} is within 0.06\% of the 195.06~J/cm\textsuperscript{2} flight value. These figures are computed by the external MDAO reference model, not by the SPARTA DSMC solver used in this work, and they establish that the IRVE-3 flight reconstruction adopted here is internally consistent. The Sutton--Graves correlation, while less accurate for this geometry, provides a conservative upper bound that is useful for TPS design margins.

The present work reports a peak heat flux of 16.14~W/cm\textsuperscript{2}, a total heat load of 165.72~J/cm\textsuperscript{2}, and a peak deceleration of 16.83\,g, i.e.\ $+12.4\%$, $-15.0\%$, and $-14.6\%$ relative to the corresponding flight measurements. The deviations are of opposite sign, have distinct causes, and must not be read as a single systematic bias. The total heat load and the peak deceleration are both \emph{resolved by the DSMC run} and both fall short by close to a common $15\%$, because the run samples a \emph{single} trajectory point ($h = 51.8$\,km, Mach 10.29, $V = 3{,}378$\,m/s) under ISA conditions whereas the flight reconstruction and the MDAO models integrate the heating and deceleration history over the complete reentry; integrating over the full trajectory raises both the accumulated heat load and the peak deceleration towards the measured values. The heat flux behaves oppositely: the 16.14~W/cm\textsuperscript{2} headline figure is the Sutton--Graves correlation evaluated at the DSMC run condition and is not a quantity resolved by the solver, and it over-predicts the flight value because the correlation is built on a spherical nose cap, carrying neither the heat-shielding effect of the scalloped torus nor the rarefied-flow departure that the DSMC solver resolves. It therefore acts as a conservative upper bound on the stagnation flux, which is the useful direction for TPS sizing. The ballistic coefficient provides an independent check: the simulated 27.70~kg/m\textsuperscript{2} is $+3\%$ relative to the 26.9~kg/m\textsuperscript{2} flight value, indicating that the scalloped geometry is reproduced correctly, so that the residual in the two integrated quantities lies in the trajectory integration while the residual in the heat flux lies in the correlation model.

\paragraph{A second Sutton--Graves value, and why it differs.}
A reader checking the validation export \texttt{unified\_comparison\_data.json} will find a \emph{different} Sutton--Graves heat flux for the same run: 12.2~W/cm\textsuperscript{2} rather than the 16.14~W/cm\textsuperscript{2} of Table~\ref{tab:validation}. This is not a contradiction, and it is worth stating precisely because both numbers are Sutton--Graves evaluations. Sutton--Graves scales as $\sqrt{\rho/R_N}\,V^3$, so the value is set entirely by the $(V, R_N)$ pair supplied to the correlation, and the two reports use different pairs. The 16.14~W/cm\textsuperscript{2} quoted above is evaluated at the IRVE-3 baseline nose radius $R_N = 1.5$\,m and at the run's own velocity $V = 3{,}378$\,m/s, which is the same baseline condition used for the before/after comparison in Sec.~\ref{sec:bo_results}. The 12.2~W/cm\textsuperscript{2} in the export is evaluated at the engine's \emph{default} nose radius $R_n = 0.55$\,m and at a nominal $V = 2{,}700$\,m/s. The repository's own validation report lists both of those as known sources of discrepancy, since it notes that ``SG uses $R_n = 0.55$\,m (default), but effective HIAD nose radius is larger (blunter body)'' and separately flags the velocity mismatch, so the 12.2~W/cm\textsuperscript{2} figure is the value produced when two acknowledged-wrong defaults are left in place, and it is not conditioned on the run's actual velocity or geometry. The value used in this thesis is the better-conditioned of the two, and the export's \texttt{root\_causes.heat\_flux\_comparison\_notes} block reaches the same conclusion about how the number should be read, stating that the correct comparison is against the analytical models \emph{at the same trajectory point}, not against a trajectory-integrated flight peak. The same block also records that the DSMC quantity actually resolved by the solver is the per-element average of 56.6~W/cm\textsuperscript{2}, which is a whole-surface mean and is not a stagnation-point peak. Consequently the $+12.4\%$ in Table~\ref{tab:validation} should be read as an order-of-magnitude scale comparison between a single-point correlation and a trajectory-integrated flight peak, not as a validated error bound.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Heating Augmentation and TPS Implications}
The simulation confirms that the scalloped toroidal geometry creates localized hot spots at the toroid crests. Comparing the scalloped and smooth runs at the same trajectory condition, the scalloped configuration raises the peak heat flux by 15.2\% and the per-element average by only 4.0\%, while the drag coefficient increases by 9.5\%; the total heat load and peak deceleration are unchanged. This distribution of the augmentation over a small crest area, rather than over the whole surface, is the physical signature expected from the inverse-square-root radius scaling ($\dot{q} \propto 1/\sqrt{R}$) of the Sutton--Graves correlation~\cite{sutton1971gravesh}. The recirculation zones in the valleys provide partial thermal shielding, reducing the time-averaged heat load on the Flexible Thermal Protection System~\cite{hollis2018surface}.

\subsection{Flow Regime Characterization}
Figure~\ref{fig:knudsen_map} demonstrates a systematic \emph{local} regime gradient around the HIAD at 51.8\,km altitude. The body surface and forward stagnation region are near-continuum, while the shock layer and wake rise into the slip-flow band. Reading the field against the five bands of Table~\ref{tab:knudsen_regimes}, the visible range of $10^{-5}$ to about $1.15\times10^{-2}$ lies entirely within the continuum and slip-flow bands: the transitional band ($0.1 \lesssim Kn \lesssim 10$) and the free-molecular band ($Kn > 10$) are \emph{not} attained at this trajectory point, and the accompanying panel key, which labels the red wake as kinetic dominance above $Kn = 0.1$, overstates what the plotted range contains and must be read as a color-scale artifact rather than as a regime boundary.

This local gradient is nonetheless sufficient to justify the DSMC choice, and the reasoning is local rather than global. Because the shock layer (the region that dominates convective heating) reaches the slip-flow band, where continuum Navier--Stokes is no longer valid without velocity and temperature slip corrections at the wall, a kinetic treatment is required exactly where the heat load is computed~\cite{mos2006lowdensity}. The stagnation region, by contrast, remains continuum, which is what licenses the continuum Navier--Stokes PINN stage. A single vehicle-scale $Kn$ evaluated on the 3\,m aeroshell diameter would place the whole vehicle in the continuum band and would discard this distinction; the local field is therefore the physically meaningful diagnostic, and it is why a hybrid DSMC-plus-PINN architecture is appropriate rather than redundant.

\subsection{Recirculation and Shock-Boundary Layer Interaction}
The velocity vector analysis reveals stable recirculation zones in the toroidal valleys, which are qualitatively consistent with the shock-boundary layer interaction (SBLI) patterns observed in hypersonic blunt-body flows~\cite{guo2019hypersonic}. The DSMC method captures these phenomena through direct molecular collision statistics, without requiring empirical turbulence closures.

\subsection{Real-Gas Effects}
The 5-species air model captures vibrational excitation and dissociation reactions that reduce the effective stagnation temperature below the calorically perfect prediction of 5952\,K. This is critical for accurate heat flux prediction, as the energy partitioning into internal modes directly affects the temperature gradient driving surface heat transfer~\cite{AndersonHypersonic}.

\section{Optimization Framework Results}
\label{sec:optimization_results}

The survivability optimization framework (Sec.~\ref{sec:ga_opt}) was executed across multiple runs to explore the HIAD design space using Latin Hypercube Sampling (LHS), Bayesian Optimization (GP Matérn 5/2 + Expected Improvement) surrogate training, and Genetic Algorithm (GA) optimization. This section presents the findings from the optimization campaign conducted between July and August 2026.

\subsection{Optimization Campaign Summary}
A total of 28 optimization runs were executed, with 25 sample evaluations recorded in the optimization database. Three runs (Run~11, Run~26, Run~27) produced complete sample sets with flight metrics. The design parameters varied include aeroshell diameter ($D$), cone half-angle ($\theta_c$), toroid count ($N$), nose radius ($R_N$), and TPS thickness ($\delta_{TPS}$), consistent with the parameter space defined in Sec.~\ref{sec:ga_opt}.

The results reveal a clear trade-off between deceleration capability and structural loading. Configurations with higher ballistic coefficient ($\beta > 25$\,kg/m$^2$) achieve moderate\,g-loads ($n_G \approx 21$--$23g$), while configurations with $\beta < 15$\,kg/m$^2$ produce excessive deceleration loads ($n_G > 55g$) that exceed structural limits.

\subsection{Thermal Constraint Violation}
A critical finding is that the vehicle does not survive the thermal constraint. With the active thermal protection system the engine reports a backface temperature of 955.66\,K, which exceeds the Kapton limit of 773\,K by 182.7\,K and the IRVE-3 flexible-TPS structural limit of 523.15\,K by 432.5\,K ~\cite{old2013irve3postflight}. The active system is not a homogeneous slab but the five-ply IRVE-3 flexible TPS: two 0.60\,mm Nextel BF-20 outer fabric plies, two 2.50\,mm Pyrogel 2250 insulation plies, and one 0.15\,mm aluminized Kapton/Kevlar gas barrier~\cite{guidotti2023efesto2materials}, totalling 6.35\,mm with a series thermal resistance of $R = 0.25605$\,m$^2$\,K/W and an outer emissivity of 0.80. This supersedes the 25.4\,mm homogeneous silicon-carbide slab of the LOFTID baseline, which was the LOFTID baseline and was four times too thick for this vehicle. The survivability verdict is set by the four-way predicate implemented in the engine, which requires the surface temperature to stay below 2073\,K, the backface temperature below 773\,K, and both the measured and computed deceleration below 25\,g. The thermal channel is therefore the binding constraint, while the deceleration channel passes with margin.

The magnitude of the exceedance indicates that the thermal soak is dominated by the integrated heat load rather than by the instantaneous stagnation heat flux. The integrated load for this trajectory point is $Q = 165.72$\,J/cm$^2$, which is $1.657$\,MJ/m$^2$; the 1D transient model integrates the heat pulse over the exposure duration, so it is this accumulated quantity, and not the stagnation-point correlation value of Chapter~3, that sets the backface temperature. This finding has direct implications for TPS design:
\begin{itemize}
    \item The active 6.35\,mm layup is already insufficient, so the insulation must be thickened beyond its present Pyrogel content. The resistance requirement follows directly from the exceedance: reducing the backface temperature from 955.66\,K to the 773\,K Kapton limit requires the series resistance to rise by a factor of roughly 2.1 at the observed temperature difference, which for this stack means increasing the low-conductivity Pyrogel plies rather than the outer fabric.
    \item The thermal lag factor ($\eta_{lag} = 0.15$) is a fixed lumped parameter rather than a fitted one, and it should be recalibrated against a higher-fidelity transient solution before the thickness recommendation above is treated as final.
    \item Multi-layer insulation with contact resistance as in Rapisarda's F-TPS stack~\cite{rapisarda2023mdao} should be incorporated.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Optimal Configuration Identification}
Despite the thermal constraint violation, the GA identified the GA-optimized configuration (Run~27, Sample~8) as the optimal design point: $D = 3.0$\,m, $\theta_c = 45^\circ$, $N = 6$, $R_N = 0.5$\,m. This configuration achieves $\beta = 29.27$\,kg/m$^2$ and $n_G = 21.37g$, which are within acceptable ranges for cargo reentry. The convergence of the optimizer toward a near-IRVE-3 geometry validates the physical consistency of the framework, as the IRVE-3 design represents a flight-tested configuration optimized through decades of NASA research~\cite{old2013irve3postflight}.

The physical interpretation follows directly from the correlation. Since $\dot{q} \propto 1/\sqrt{R_N}$ in the Sutton--Graves scaling~\cite{sutton1971gravesh}, nearly doubling the nose radius is the cheapest available route to a large heat-load reduction; the toroid radius and half-cone angle are then reduced to recover the drag and ballistic-coefficient terms that the enlarged nose would otherwise penalize. The optimizer thus discovers the same trade-off that a designer would reach analytically, which is a useful consistency check on the cost function.

\section{Hybrid Validation Gate}
\label{sec:hybrid_gate}

\subsection{Motivation}
An analytic optimum is only meaningful if it survives re-simulation at full fidelity. The surrogate $J(\mathbf{x})$ is a cheap surrogate built on closed-form correlations; it cannot represent the particle noise, real-gas chemistry, or geometric discretization effects present in the DSMC solution. A surrogate that is systematically optimistic would therefore select a design that fails when actually flown. The hybrid validation gate closes this loop by re-simulating the winner with the high-fidelity chain before its acceptance.

\subsection{Gate Protocol}
The gate is enforced as a post-optimization stage and executes five legs~\cite{kennedy2000multi}:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Preconditions and geometry QA.} The compiled binary, the Docker daemon, the validation virtual environment, the pipeline module, and the reference CSV are all checked, followed by a seconds-fast \texttt{\seqsplit{bin/main -{}-validate-only}} geometry check. Every failure is reported with a path and a remedy, and the gate exits with status 1.
    \item \textbf{DSMC leg.} The round-1 winner is re-simulated with \texttt{\seqsplit{bin/main -{}-validate -{}-headless -{}-nose 2.971440 -{}-tradius 0.088460 -{}-angle 40.147949 -{}-skin scalloped -{}-steps 2200}}, writing to a dedicated results directory. The reference dataset used for the baseline comparison is never overwritten.
    \item \textbf{PINN leg.} The PINN refinement pipeline is executed on the freshly written dataset, producing a new unified comparison dataset under the gate directory.
    \item \textbf{Verdict.} The gate passes if and only if $q_{hybrid} \leq Q_{target} = 165.716$\,J/cm$^2$, and it records the prediction error $(q_{hybrid} - q_{analytic})/q_{analytic}$.
    \item \textbf{Multi-fidelity correction.} A bounded correction factor $c = \operatorname{clamp}\bigl(q_{hybrid}/\allowbreak q_{analytic},\,\allowbreak 0.5,\,\allowbreak 2.0\bigr)$ is applied to the heat-load term of $J(\mathbf{x})$ and the optimizer is re-run, producing the corrected round-2 design.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Gate Result}
The official gate execution (2026-09-26) returns \textsc{pass}. The analytic prediction for the round-1 winner was $q_{analytic} = 155.67$\,J/cm$^2$; the high-fidelity DSMC re-simulation returned
\begin{equation}
    q_{hybrid} = 77.93\ \text{J/cm}^2 \;\leq\; Q_{target} = 165.72\ \text{J/cm}^2,
\end{equation}
with a prediction error of $-49.94\,\%$ and a clamped correction factor $c = 0.5006$, which sits exactly at the lower clamp bound. The hybrid drag coefficient is $C_{d,hybrid} = 2.529$.

Two properties of this result deserve emphasis. First, the winner passes with substantial margin, so the round-1 design is genuinely safe rather than marginally acceptable. Second, the $-49.94\,\%$ error is not noise: two independent DSMC runs of the same geometry agreed to within 0.3\,\%, so the analytic-to-hybrid gap is reproducible systematic bias in the closed-form surrogate~\cite{kennedy2000multi}, precisely the quantity the multi-fidelity correction is designed to absorb. This is the expected behavior of a corrector that is being given a real signal to learn from, rather than an error to be hidden.

Re-running the optimizer with the corrected cost function yields a second optimum at $R_N = 2.9979$\,m, $r_{tor} = 0.0530$\,m, $\theta_c = 42.29^\circ$, with $C_d = 1.5440$ and a corrected cost of $J = 1.1415$ (down from an uncorrected $1.6085$ at the same point). \section{Comparative Analysis with Prior Work}
\label{sec:comparison_rapisarda}
A direct comparison between the present framework and the prior MDAO framework~\cite{rapisarda2023mdao} highlights the methodological advances introduced in this research. While both frameworks target HIAD survivability optimization using the IRVE-3 geometry as a baseline, the underlying computational approaches differ fundamentally.

\subsection{Aerodynamic Solver}
Rapisarda's MDAO framework employs a Modified Newtonian panel method for aerodynamic coefficient estimation~\cite{rapisarda2023mdao}. This is an inviscid, continuum-based algebraic method that approximates the pressure coefficient on surface panels using the local deflection angle. While computationally efficient, the Newtonian assumption is strictly valid only for hypersonic, high-density flows ($Kn < 0.001$) where the shock layer is thin and attached to the surface~\cite{AndersonHypersonic}.

In contrast, the present framework uses the SPARTA Direct Simulation Monte Carlo (DSMC) solver~\cite{plimpton2014sparta}, which solves the Boltzmann equation through direct simulation of molecular collisions. DSMC is physically valid across all flow regimes ($Kn = 0$ to $\infty$), so no regime bound restricts its applicability~\cite{bird1994molecular}. What the local $Kn$ field of Figure~\ref{fig:knudsen_map} establishes is narrower and more specific: at 51.8\,km the shock layer around the HIAD reaches the slip-flow band ($0.01 \lesssim Kn \lesssim 0.1$), where continuum Navier--Stokes requires slip corrections at the wall, while the freestream and stagnation region remain in the continuum band. DSMC is therefore required precisely in the region that carries the convective heat load, rather than uniformly across the vehicle.

\subsection{Gas Model}
The Newtonian method assumes a calorically perfect gas with constant specific heats ($\gamma = 1.4$ for air). At hypersonic speeds ($Ma > 5$), the post-shock temperature exceeds 5000\,K, activating vibrational excitation, molecular dissociation, and ionization~\cite{AndersonHypersonic}. The present framework employs a 5-species air model (N$_2$, O$_2$, NO, N, O) with Variable Soft Sphere (VSS) collision physics, capturing these real-gas effects that alter the energy partitioning and reduce the effective stagnation temperature below the calorically perfect prediction.

\subsection{Optimization Methodology}
Rapisarda's framework uses a sequential, single-objective approach: the aerodynamic coefficients are computed at each design point, and the trajectory and thermal analysis are performed independently~\cite{rapisarda2023mdao}. The design space exploration relies on manual parametric sweeps with individual Newtonian panel computations.

The present framework implements a Genetic Algorithm (GA) coupled with a Bayesian Optimization (GP Matérn 5/2 + Expected Improvement) surrogate model for multi-objective optimization. Latin Hypercube Sampling (LHS) generates the initial design population, and the Bayes Opt (GP) surrogate enables rapid evaluation of thousands of candidate geometries. This approach is essential for exploring the 6+ dimensional design space (aeroshell diameter, cone angle, toroid count, toroid radius, nose radius, and grid factor), which would be computationally prohibitive with individual SPARTA simulations.

\subsection{Physics-Informed Neural Network Refinement}
A novel contribution of the present work is the application of Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) as a post-processing layer for DSMC data~\cite{raissi2019pinn}. The DeepXDE-based PINN solves the 2D compressible Navier--Stokes equations using automatic differentiation, with SPARTA grid data introduced as observational boundary conditions. This provides a smooth, physics-consistent interpolant that fills gaps in the DSMC flow field and reduces particle noise. This hybrid PINN-DSMC approach is absent from Rapisarda's framework and represents a new paradigm for rarefied gas dynamics post-processing.

\subsection{Reproducibility and Accessibility}
The present framework is fully open-source and built on a hybrid Ada and Python architecture. The Ada binary is the primary simulation engine, and its analytical, geometric and flight-metric subprograms carry range-checked parameter subtypes and run-time assertions that are exercised by the engine self-test; it is driven through the \texttt{stellarorion\_program\_proc/run.py} command-line front end, which supersedes the now-removed \texttt{main.py} entry point. The SPARTA simulation runs inside a containerized Linux environment for reproducibility, while the Python sidecar handles PINN refinement, optimization, and the GUI on the native OS with support for global hardware acceleration (NVIDIA CUDA, AMD ROCm, Apple Metal MPS). Rapisarda's framework is MATLAB-based and requires manual configuration for each simulation run.
