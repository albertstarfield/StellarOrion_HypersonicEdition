\textbf{\large  ABSTRACT}\\[0.5cm]
\end{center}

This thesis investigates the aerothermal characteristics of Hypersonic Inflatable Aerodynamic Decelerators (HIADs), focusing on the effect of their scalloped surface topology on flow physics and aerodynamic heating. Using the Direct Simulation Monte Carlo (DSMC) method in the SPARTA solver, the study builds a framework for rarefied gas dynamics analysis of HIAD reentry over the Knudsen number range $0.01 < Kn < 1$. The framework integrates CadQuery geometry generation, DSMC aerothermal simulation, Physics-Informed Neural Network (PINN) refinement, and Genetic Algorithm Optimization with a Bayesian Optimization surrogate, a Gaussian Process (GP) with a Matérn 5/2 kernel and an Expected Improvement acquisition function. The methodology derives from the Boltzmann transport equation and is validated against IRVE-3 flight data, with the Sutton-Graves stagnation-point correlation and Fay-Riddell theory serving as thermal benchmarks. A 5-species air model (N$_2$, O$_2$, NO, N, O) with Variable Soft Sphere collision physics captures vibrational excitation and dissociation. The DSMC solution resolves a cell-scale Knudsen number field spanning nearly three decades within the continuum band at the sampled trajectory point; the slip-flow band is reached only over the millimetre bow-shock thickness, and the transitional and free-molecular bands are not reached. Under that condition the Sutton-Graves correlation gives a peak stagnation heat flux of $16.14$\,W/cm$^2$, which is $+12.4\%$ relative to the $14.36$\,W/cm$^2$ IRVE-3 flight measurement. The same peak is $+5.8\%$ relative to the $15.26$\,W/cm$^2$ trajectory-integrated peak reported for the reference multidisciplinary design analysis and optimization (MDAO) model. The integrated quantities deviate from the flight values by $-15.0\%$ for total heat load and $-14.6\%$ for peak deceleration, because the DSMC run samples a single trajectory point while the flight reconstruction and the MDAO model integrate the whole reentry. Optimization over nose radius, torus radius and half-cone angle selects a geometry whose nose radius is $2.9714$\,m. The vehicle does not survive the thermal constraint: the five-ply IRVE-3 flexible thermal protection system, $6.35$\,mm in total, yields a backface temperature of $955.66$\,K, which exceeds the $523.15$\,K structural limit of that system by $432.51$\,K. Survivability is therefore a constraint on the material stack rather than on the geometry alone, and that is the central finding of this work.

\noindent \textbf{Keywords}: Hypersonic Inflatable Aerodynamic Decelerator (HIAD), Aerothermodynamics, Direct Simulation Monte Carlo (DSMC), SPARTA, Rarefied Gas Dynamics, Scalloped Geometry, Aerodynamic Heating, Physics-Informed Neural Network (PINN), Genetic Algorithm Optimization
