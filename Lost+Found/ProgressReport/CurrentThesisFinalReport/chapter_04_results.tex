\chapter{Results and Discussion}
\label{ch:results}

\section{Overview}
This chapter presents the simulation results and discussion derived from the SPARTA DSMC computational framework described in Chapter 3. The analysis covers the baseline IRVE-3 geometry configuration, the aerothermal surface distributions, flow field characteristics, and the validation against flight data. All results are generated using the StellarOrion engine with the 6-toroid stacked HIAD geometry at the Rapisarda (2023) MDAO baseline parameters~\cite{rapisarda2023mdao}.

\section{Baseline Configuration and Flight Conditions}
The baseline simulation corresponds to the IRVE-3 reentry trajectory point at 51.8\,km altitude, with the following freestream conditions. The atmospheric quantities are the values returned by the atmospheric model evaluated at that altitude:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Freestream Velocity ($V_{\infty}$):} 3378\,m/s (Mach $\approx$ 10.29)
    \item \textbf{Freestream Density ($\rho_{\infty}$):} $7.851 \times 10^{-4}$\,kg/m$^3$ (atmospheric model, code baseline)
    \item \textbf{Freestream Temperature ($T_{\infty}$):} 268.41\,K (atmospheric model, code baseline)
    \item \textbf{Vehicle Mass ($m$):} 281\,kg
    \item \textbf{Aeroshell Diameter ($D$):} 3.0\,m
    \item \textbf{Nose Radius ($R_N$):} 1.5\,m
    \item \textbf{Toroid Count:} 6 (stacked toroid configuration)
    \item \textbf{Toroid Radius ($r_{tor}$):} 0.135\,m
    \item \textbf{Grid Factor:} 0.7 (validated optimal against IRVE-3 data)
\end{itemize}

The stagnation temperature for a calorically perfect gas at these conditions is:
\begin{equation}
    T_0 = T_{\infty} \left( 1 + \frac{\gamma - 1}{2} M_{\infty}^2 \right) = 268.41 \ \text{K} \left( 1 + 0.2 \times 105.9 \right) = 5952 \ \text{K}
\end{equation}
However, the 5-species air model (N$_2$, O$_2$, NO, N, O) with VSS collisions captures real-gas effects including vibrational excitation above $\sim$800\,K and dissociation above $\sim$2000\,K, resulting in a lower effective translational temperature~\cite{bird1994molecular}.

\section{Surface Heat Flux Distribution}
The stagnation-point heat flux is computed using the Sutton-Graves (1971) correlation~\cite{sutton1971gravesh}:
\begin{equation}
    \dot{q}_{stag} = C_{SG} \sqrt{\frac{\rho_{\infty}}{R_N}} \cdot V_{\infty}^3
\end{equation}
where $C_{SG} = 1.7415 \times 10^{-4}$\,W$\cdot$s$^2$/m$^{5}$ for Earth air in SI units, the value implemented in the Ada/SPARK model.

\subsection{Stagnation Point Results}
For the baseline IRVE-3 configuration the correlation is evaluated at the SPARTA DSMC run condition ($h = 51.8$\,km, ISA $\rho_{\infty} = 7.851 \times 10^{-4}$\,kg/m$^3$, $V_{\infty} = 3{,}378$\,m/s) with the IRVE-3 body radius $R_N = 1.5$\,m corresponding to the 3\,m aeroshell diameter:
\begin{equation}
    \begin{split}
        \dot{q}_{stag} &= 1.7415 \times 10^{-4} \times \sqrt{\frac{7.851 \times 10^{-4}}{1.5}} \times (3378)^3 \\
        &\approx 1.536 \times 10^{5} \ \text{W/m}^2 = 15.36 \ \text{W/cm}^2
    \end{split}
\end{equation}

An earlier revision of this work used $C_{SG} = 1.83 \times 10^{-4}$ and reported $16.14$\,W/cm$^2$ for the same condition, about $5\%$ higher. The optimizer evaluates the same correlation at the physical nose radius $R_n = \kappa R_N = 0.55$\,m (Eq.~\eqref{eq:nose_ratio}), which gives $25.36$\,W/cm$^2$ at the baseline, while the $12.20$\,W/cm$^2$ in the validation export is a legacy evaluation at a hardcoded $V_{\infty} = 2700$\,m/s and $\rho_{\infty} = 6.9674 \times 10^{-4}$\,kg/m$^3$. These are the same correlation at different $(R_n,V_{\infty})$ pairs, not three measurements.

The IRVE-3 flight measurement reports a peak heat flux of 14.36~W/cm\textsuperscript{2}~\cite{lau2013irve3}. Rapisarda (2023) reports the trajectory-integrated Sutton-Graves peak as 15.26~W/cm\textsuperscript{2} (+6.26\% above flight) and the Fay--Riddell CFD prediction as 13.83~W/cm\textsuperscript{2} (-3.69\% below flight)~\cite{rapisarda2023mdao}. The Fay--Riddell correlation is more physically accurate for HIAD geometries because it accounts for the actual shock layer structure, while Sutton-Graves assumes a spherical nose cap. The single-point SG value at the body radius (15.36~W/cm\textsuperscript{2}) is $+6.9\%$ above the flight value and within $+0.7\%$ of Rapisarda's trajectory-integrated peak (15.26~W/cm\textsuperscript{2}), despite the different density profiles: Rapisarda integrates a parameterized trajectory under MCD v6.1 while this work evaluates a single ISA point.

It is important to state precisely what the 15.36~W/cm\textsuperscript{2} figure represents, because it is \emph{not} a directly sampled DSMC heat flux. It is the Sutton--Graves correlation evaluated at the DSMC run condition, and it is the same class of quantity as Rapisarda's trajectory-integrated SG value. The raw DSMC surface data tell a different and noisier story. Reading the unsmoothed per-element kinetic energy flux from the \texttt{00\_reference} surface dumps yields a final per-element average of 55.2~W/cm\textsuperscript{2} and a final maximum single-cell value of 192.0~W/cm\textsuperscript{2}. The maximum is a single point-sample in a field where individual cells fluctuate by orders of magnitude --- including negative values at later time steps --- and is statistical scatter rather than a resolved physical peak. The per-element average is a true DSMC quantity but is inflated relative to the stagnation-point correlation because it averages over the whole scalloped surface, including the low-heating recirculation pockets, and because it is taken from an unsmoothed noisy field. For this reason the correlation-conditioned value is used as the headline heat-flux metric throughout, and the raw DSMC numbers are reported here only to document the noise characteristics of the surface data.

\subsection{Scalloping Effect on Local Heating}
The toroidal surface topology introduces localized variations in the radius of curvature. At each toroid crest, the local radius of curvature ($r_{tor} = 0.135$\,m) is much smaller than the global nose radius ($R_N = 1.5$\,m). Since $\dot{q} \propto 1/\sqrt{R}$ in the Sutton--Graves scaling, the correlation sets an upper bound on the geometric enhancement available at a crest:
\begin{equation}
    \left(\frac{\dot{q}_{crest}}{\dot{q}_{smooth}}\right)_{\mathrm{geometric\ bound}} = \sqrt{\frac{R_N}{r_{tor}}} = \sqrt{\frac{1.5}{0.135}} \approx 3.33
    \label{eq:crest_bound}
\end{equation}

Equation~\eqref{eq:crest_bound} is a \emph{bound} and not a prediction: it applies the $1/\sqrt{R}$ scaling to the crest radius while holding every other term of the correlation at its smooth-body value, and it neglects the concurrent increase in frontal area and the change in effective shock standoff that accompany a rippled surface. The measured augmentation is therefore substantially smaller than this bound. Comparing the scalloped and smooth DSMC solutions at the identical run condition (step 2200) gives a peak kinetic energy flux of 182.5 against 158.4~W/cm\textsuperscript{2} and a per-element average of 56.6 against 54.4~W/cm\textsuperscript{2}, i.e.\ increases of $15.2\%$ and $4.0\%$ respectively --- consistent in sign and in ordering with the geometric bound, and roughly a factor of five below it. The same comparison gives a $9.5\%$ increase in $C_d$ (1.4625 against 1.3358) and a $5.5\%$ increase in $|C_l|$, while the peak $g$-load and the heat load are unchanged because both geometries are evaluated at the same trajectory point. Those scalar kinetic energy fluxes are read from the SPARTA surface compute data (\texttt{ke}); Figure~\ref{fig:thermal_map} shows the temperature field of the same meridional slice and is a qualitative rendering, not a plot of \texttt{ke}.

\subsection{Drag Coefficient}
The drag coefficient is computed from the total axial force:
\begin{equation}
    C_d = \frac{f_x}{\frac{1}{2} \rho_{\infty} V_{\infty}^2 A_{ref}}
\end{equation}
where $A_{ref} = \pi (D/2)^2$ is the reference area based on the aeroshell diameter. The HIAD's large frontal area produces a high drag coefficient, which is the fundamental mechanism enabling deceleration at high altitudes where atmospheric density is low~\cite{anderson2006hypersonic}.

\subsection{Knudsen Number Distribution}
The Knudsen number field (Figure~\ref{fig:knudsen_map}) maps the flow regime transition across the computational domain:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Near-Wall (Stagnation):} $Kn < 0.01$ (near-continuum)
    \item \textbf{Shock Layer:} $0.01 < Kn < 1$ (transitional flow, DSMC regime)
    \item \textbf{Freestream:} $Kn > 1$ (free-molecular flow)
\end{itemize}

These thresholds are ordered from the wall outwards, and only the first is exercised by Figure~\ref{fig:knudsen_map}: the freestream $Kn$ at the $2700$\,m/s condition of that panel is well below 1, so the panel shows continuum and near-continuum flow only and must not be cited as evidence of free-molecular flow.

This distribution validates the use of DSMC for the majority of the flow field, as the transitional regime dominates the shock layer and near-wall regions at 51.8\,km altitude~\cite{plimpton2014sparta}.

The MDAO Fay--Riddell prediction of 13.83~W/cm\textsuperscript{2} is within 3.7\% of the 14.36~W/cm\textsuperscript{2} flight measurement, and its total heat load of 195.17~J/cm\textsuperscript{2} is within 0.06\% of the 195.06~J/cm\textsuperscript{2} flight value. These figures are computed by the external MDAO reference model, not by the SPARTA DSMC solver used in this work, and they establish that the IRVE-3 flight reconstruction adopted here is internally consistent.

The present work's DSMC run reports a total heat load of 165.72~J/cm\textsuperscript{2} ($-15.0\%$), a peak deceleration of 22.72\,$g$ ($+15.3\%$), a final drag force of 45{,}613~N and a body-area drag coefficient of 0.4414. The two integrated quantities err in opposite directions and have distinct causes. The heat load is low because the run samples a single trajectory point under ISA conditions whereas the flight reconstruction integrates the heating history over the complete entry. The deceleration peak is high because the run is conditioned at the high-dynamic-pressure point and its maximum occurs in the early transient (step 100), which is a different instant from the flight peak. Neither deviation is a single systematic bias, and neither is corrected here.

The peak heat flux in Table~\ref{tab:validation} is a Sutton--Graves evaluation, not a quantity resolved by the solver: 15.36~W/cm\textsuperscript{2} at $R=1.5$\,m and $V_{\infty}=3{,}378$\,m/s with $C_{SG}=1.7415\times10^{-4}$. The analytic model used by the optimizer evaluates the same correlation at the physical nose radius $R_n=\kappa R_N$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:nose_ratio} ($0.55$\,m at the baseline), which gives 25.36~W/cm\textsuperscript{2}; the $12.20$~W/cm\textsuperscript{2} in the validation export is a legacy evaluation at $V_{\infty}=2{,}700$\,m/s. The three values are the same correlation at different $(R_n,V_{\infty})$ pairs, and none of them is a stagnation-point sample of the DSMC field. The $+6.9\%$ in Table~\ref{tab:validation} is therefore a scale comparison between a single-point correlation and a trajectory-integrated flight peak, not a validated error bound.

\section{TPS Conduction, Re-radiation and Lateral Coupling}

\section{Convergence and Residuals}
The DSMC simulation convergence is monitored through residual tracking (Figure~\ref{fig:convergence}). Each curve plots the normalised residual $|x - x_{\mathrm{ref}}|/|x_{\mathrm{ref}}|$ of a monitored quantity against the particle-time step, where $x_{\mathrm{ref}}$ is the mean of the final three samples and is taken as the converged level. All three residuals fall by roughly three orders of magnitude between step 100 and step 2200, which is the statistical steady-state expected of a particle method~\cite{bird1994molecular}. The approach is not monotonic: because each estimate is a finite-sample mean over the particles present in the cell, the residuals retain visible scatter (most conspicuously in the heat flux, which fluctuates by a factor of two between adjacent samples late in the run) as the statistics accumulate. The monotone trend is therefore a property of the trend, not of individual samples.

\section{Discussion of the DSMC Results}

\subsection{Heating Augmentation and TPS Implications}
The simulation confirms that the scalloped toroidal geometry creates localized hot spots at the toroid crests. Comparing the scalloped and smooth runs at the same trajectory condition, the scalloped configuration raises the peak heat flux by 15.2\% and the per-element average by only 4.0\%, while the drag coefficient increases by 9.5\%; the total heat load and peak deceleration are unchanged. This distribution of the augmentation over a small crest area, rather than over the whole surface, is the physical signature expected from the inverse-square-root radius scaling ($\dot{q} \propto 1/\sqrt{R}$) of the Sutton-Graves correlation~\cite{sutton1971gravesh}. The recirculation zones in the valleys provide partial thermal shielding, reducing the time-averaged heat load on the Flexible Thermal Protection System (FTPS)~\cite{hollis2018surface}.

\subsection{Flow Regime Characterization}
The Knudsen number field map demonstrates that the flow around the HIAD at 51.8\,km altitude spans all three regimes: transitional in the shock layer, and near-continuum at the stagnation region. This confirms the necessity of using DSMC rather than continuum CFD for accurate aerothermal prediction at these conditions~\cite{mos2006lowdensity}.

\subsection{Recirculation and Shock-Boundary Layer Interaction}
The velocity vector analysis reveals stable recirculation zones in the toroidal valleys, which are qualitatively consistent with the shock-boundary layer interaction (SBLI) patterns observed in hypersonic blunt-body flows~\cite{guo2019hypersonic}. The DSMC method captures these phenomena through direct molecular collision statistics, without requiring empirical turbulence closures.

\subsection{Real-Gas Effects}
The 5-species air model captures vibrational excitation and dissociation reactions that reduce the effective stagnation temperature below the calorically perfect prediction of 5952\,K. This is critical for accurate heat flux prediction, as the energy partitioning into internal modes directly affects the temperature gradient driving surface heat transfer~\cite{anderson2006hypersonic}.

\subsection{Pass-1 Outcome and the Gate}
The pass-1 candidate does not survive the high-fidelity check: its DSMC heat load is $175.580$\,J/cm\textsuperscript{2} against the $165.716$\,J/cm\textsuperscript{2} target. \subsection{Interpretation of the Trade-Off}
The first pass's result is a $+2.14\,\%$ gain in drag coefficient bought with a +5.96\,\% rise in the screening heat load, for a $9.19\,\%$ reduction in composite cost. The gain is not free: it is bought by a smaller body ($D = 2.6719$\,m against the IRVE-3 $3.0$\,m) and a blunter nose ($R_n = 0.4898$\,m against $0.55$\,m), which is exactly the direction the drag-reward term of $J(\mathbf{x})$ pays for.

The first pass is nevertheless not a thermally admissible design, and the gate of Sec.~\ref{sec:hybrid_gate} says so: its DSMC re-simulation returns $q_{hybrid} = 175.580$ J/cm\textsuperscript{2} against $Q_{target} = 165.716$, so the pass-1 candidate \emph{fails} the heat gate, and the analytic screening model over-predicts the load by $2.08$ ($364.87$ against $175.580$ J/cm\textsuperscript{2}, a $51.88\,\%$ prediction error). That failure is the purpose of the second pass: the solver's verdict sets the bounded correction factor and the GA is re-run with it, and the validated outcome is the pass-2 geometry reported in Sec.~\ref{sec:hybrid_gate}.

\section{Comparative Analysis with Prior Work}
\label{sec:comparison_rapisarda}
A direct comparison between the present framework and the prior MDAO framework of Rapisarda (2023)~\cite{rapisarda2023mdao} highlights the methodological advances introduced in this research. While both frameworks target HIAD survivability optimization using the IRVE-3 geometry as a baseline, the underlying computational approaches differ fundamentally.

\subsection{Aerodynamic Solver}
Rapisarda's MDAO framework employs a Modified Newtonian panel method for aerodynamic coefficient estimation~\cite{rapisarda2023mdao}. This is an inviscid, continuum-based algebraic method that approximates the pressure coefficient on surface panels using the local deflection angle. While computationally efficient, the Newtonian assumption is strictly valid only for hypersonic, high-density flows ($Kn < 0.001$) where the shock layer is thin and attached to the surface~\cite{anderson2006hypersonic}.

In contrast, the present framework uses the SPARTA Direct Simulation Monte Carlo (DSMC) solver~\cite{plimpton2014sparta}, which solves the Boltzmann equation through direct simulation of molecular collisions. DSMC is physically valid across all flow regimes ($Kn = 0$ to $\infty$), including the transitional regime ($0.01 < Kn < 1$) encountered at reentry altitudes above 50\,km where the continuum assumption breaks down~\cite{bird1994molecular}. This is evidenced by the Knudsen number field map (Figure~\ref{fig:knudsen_map}), which shows that the shock layer around the HIAD at 51.8\,km altitude stays in the transitional to near-continuum range, with local Knudsen numbers between $10^{-5}$ and $1.2\times10^{-2}$ and no free-molecular region.

\subsection{Gas Model}
The Newtonian method assumes a calorically perfect gas with constant specific heats ($\gamma = 1.4$ for air). At hypersonic speeds ($Ma > 5$), the post-shock temperature exceeds 5000\,K, activating vibrational excitation, molecular dissociation, and ionization~\cite{anderson2006hypersonic}. The present framework employs a 5-species air model (N$_2$, O$_2$, NO, N, O) with Variable Soft Sphere (VSS) collision physics, capturing these real-gas effects that alter the energy partitioning and reduce the effective stagnation temperature below the calorically perfect prediction.

\subsection{Optimization Methodology}
Rapisarda's framework uses a sequential, single-objective approach: the aerodynamic coefficients are computed at each design point, and the trajectory and thermal analysis are performed independently~\cite{rapisarda2023mdao}. The design space exploration relies on manual parametric sweeps with individual Newtonian panel computations.

The present framework implements a simple Genetic Algorithm (GA) on an analytic cost: a population of fifty designs is seeded by a Latin hypercube and evolved with tournament selection, uniform crossover, Gaussian mutation and elitism. Because the cost is closed-form, thousands of candidate geometries are evaluated without a solver call, and the high-fidelity DSMC chain is reserved for the reference validation and the final verification rather than for the search loop. This approach is essential for exploring the five effective coordinates (body radius, torus radius, half-cone angle, toroid count and mass) with thousands of evaluations, which would be computationally prohibitive if every candidate required an individual SPARTA simulation.

\subsection{Reproducibility and Accessibility}
The present framework is fully open-source, Python-based, and Docker-containerized. The SPARTA simulation runs in a reproducible Linux environment, while the Python optimization pipeline runs on the native OS and evaluates the analytic cost of Eq.~\eqref{eq:ga_cost} in closed form. Rapisarda's framework is MATLAB-based and requires manual configuration for each simulation run.
